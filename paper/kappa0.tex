\title[A horn-map constant for Mandelbrot satellite bulbs]{A parabolic-renormalization constant\\ governing the shape of the $1/q$ bulbs of the Mandelbrot set}
\author{Lars Bendixen}
\date{September 2026}
\subjclass[2020]{Primary 37F46; Secondary 37F25, 37F10}
\keywords{Mandelbrot set, satellite components, holomorphic index, r\'esidu it\'eratif, parabolic implosion, horn map, near-parabolic renormalization}

\begin{abstract}
To first order, the satellite component of the Mandelbrot set attached to the main cardioid at internal angle $p/q$ is a round disc of diameter proportional to $q^{-2}$, the Ford-circle law. We study the first correction. Writing the multiplier of the bifurcating $q$-cycle as a power series in the rescaled parameter $u=q^2\varepsilon$, $\lambda=e^{2\pi i p/q}e^{\varepsilon}$, we prove that its quadratic coefficient is $\kappa(p/q)=(\iota_{p/q}-\tfrac12)/q$, where $\iota_{p/q}$ is the holomorphic index of the parabolic fixed point of $f^q$, $f(z)=e^{2\pi ip/q}z+z^2$, and that $\kappa(p/q)+\tfrac12$ is itself the index of the $q$-fold rotation quotient of $f^q$. An exact normal-form recursion evaluates $\kappa$ in $O(q^2)$ operations without loss of precision. For $p=1$ we find
\[
\kappa_0:=\lim_{q\to\infty}\kappa(1/q)=0.023825887402200569000\ldots-0.052304659114003666317\ldots\,i,
\]
with $\kappa(1/q)=\kappa_0-\tfrac{i}{2\pi q}+O(q^{-2})$, and we identify $\kappa_0$, to 27 digits, with $\iota(\PP)-\tfrac12$, where $\PP$ is the parabolic renormalization (upper horn map) of $z+z^2$. From the horn map we compute $\kappa_0$ to 32 digits. The same horn map, multiplied by the Inou--Shishikura phase $e^{-2\pi i/\alpha}$, predicts the bounded-$p$ limits of $\kappa$ and of the bulb size itself; in degree three the horn map of the cubic cusp germ plays the same role. No integer relation with standard constants is found.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let $\lambda\in\D$ parametrize the main cardioid of the Mandelbrot set $M$ by the multiplier of the attracting fixed point, $c(\lambda)=\lambda/2-\lambda^2/4$; equivalently we work with the family
\[
f_\lambda(z)=\lambda z+z^2 ,
\]
which is affinely conjugate to $z^2+c(\lambda)$. At each root of unity $\lambda_0=e^{2\pi i p/q}$, $\gcd(p,q)=1$, a satellite hyperbolic component $W_{p/q}$ of period $q$ is attached to the cardioid~\cite{DH}. Its size is of order $q^{-2}$~\cite{GM}; more precisely, to first order in $1/q$ the component is a round disc whose diameter is $2|c'(\lambda_0)|q^{-2}=2\sin(\pi p/q)\,q^{-2}$ in the parameter plane, an estimate derived analytically by Fowler and McGuinness~\cite{FM}. The diameters $q^{-2}$ and the tangency pattern of the $W_{p/q}$ are those of the Ford circles~\cite{Ford}, and this is the leading-order content of the familiar analogy between the Mandelbrot set and the Farey tessellation.

This paper is about the \emph{first correction} to that law, which to our knowledge has not been studied before, and about one constant that it produces. Put $\lambda=\lambda_0e^{\varepsilon}$ and let $\rho_{p/q}(\varepsilon)$ be the multiplier of the $q$-cycle of $f_\lambda$ that bifurcates from the fixed point $0$. In the bulb-scale variable $u=q^2\varepsilon$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R}
R_{p/q}(u):=\rho_{p/q}(u/q^2)=\sum_{k\ge0} r_k(p/q)\,u^k .
\end{equation}
The component $W_{p/q}$ is, up to the conformal change $u\mapsto c(\lambda_0e^{u/q^2})$, the set $\{|R_{p/q}(u)|<1\}$. We show that $r_0=1$ and $r_1=-1$ for every $p/q$; hence to first order $W_{p/q}$ is the disc $|u-1|<1$, which is the Ford law. The first coefficient that sees the arithmetic of $p/q$ is
\[
\kappa(p/q):=r_2(p/q).
\]

\subsection*{Results}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Index formula} (Theorem~\ref{thm:index}). $\kappa(p/q)=(\iota_{p/q}-\tfrac12)/q$, where $\iota_{p/q}=\Res_{z=0}\,dz/(z-f_{\lambda_0}^q(z))$ is the holomorphic index of the parabolic fixed point. In particular $\kappa(p/q)$ lies in the cyclotomic field $\Q(\zeta_q)$ and $p\mapsto\kappa(p/q)$ is a Galois orbit.
\item \emph{Normal form} (Theorem~\ref{thm:nf} and Corollary~\ref{cor:quotient}). If $N(z)=\lambda_0z(1+b_1z^q+b_2z^{2q})$ is the resonant normal form of $f_{\lambda_0}$, then $q\,\iota_{p/q}=(q^2-1)/2+b_2/b_1^2$. Moreover $\kappa(p/q)=\iota(\Phi_{p/q})-\tfrac12$, where $\Phi_{p/q}(W)=N^q(W^{1/q})^q$ is the rotation quotient of $f^q$. The recursion for $b_1,b_2$ gives $\kappa$ exactly in $O(q^2)$ operations and is free of cancellation.
\item \emph{The constant} (Numerical result~\ref{obs:limit}). The limit $\kappa_0=\lim_{q\to\infty}\kappa(1/q)$ exists numerically, with $\kappa(1/q)=\kappa_0-\tfrac{i}{2\pi q}+O(q^{-2})$; the bulbs determine $\kappa_0$ to 27 digits.
\item \emph{Identification} (Theorem~\ref{thm:horn-index} and Numerical result~\ref{obs:identity}). Let $E(Z)=Z+\sum_{n\ge1}a_ne^{2\pi i nZ}$ be the upper horn map of $z+z^2$ and $\PP(W)=W\exp(2\pi i\sum a_nW^n)$ the corresponding parabolic germ. Then $\iota(\PP)-\tfrac12=a_2/(2\pi i a_1^2)$, and this number equals $\kappa_0$ to 27 digits. From the horn map,
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{Re}\kappa_0&=\phantom{-}0.023825887402200569000729189645402\ldots,\\
\operatorname{Im}\kappa_0&=-0.052304659114003666316522486207571\ldots .
\end{align*}
\item \emph{Renormalization} (Section~\ref{sec:renorm}). The identification is one instance of a broader picture in which the rotation quotient $\Phi_{p/q}$ is a near-parabolic renormalization of $f_{p/q}$. This picture predicts, with no free parameters, the limits of $\kappa(p/q)$ and of the bulb size along every sequence of bounded $p$, and the analogous constants for $z^3+c$. The predicted values of $\kappa$ agree with the bulb data to $10^{-8}$ in degree two and $5\cdot10^{-6}$ in degree three, and the predicted bulb sizes to $9\cdot10^{-7}$. Iterating the construction along continued fractions with large partial quotients gives a hierarchy $\kappa_0=\kappa_1,\kappa_2,\ldots$ that contracts by a factor $0.042$ per level towards a second constant $\kappa_*=0.011929183413-0.037455493752\,i$; the second and third levels are confirmed on bulbs, to $3\cdot10^{-10}$ and $1.2\cdot10^{-6}$.
\end{enumerate}

Items (1) and (2) are theorems. Items (3)--(5) are numerical results, obtained with rigorous or cross-validated arithmetic and stated with their error estimates. We do not prove the identity $\kappa_0=\iota(\PP)-\frac12$; Section~\ref{sec:renorm} explains why we expect a proof along the lines of the Inou--Shishikura theory of near-parabolic renormalization~\cite{IS}.

\subsection*{Why this constant matters}
The number $\kappa_0$ controls the shape of the $1/q$-bulbs, which accumulate at the cusp $c=\tfrac14$ of $M$; for instance the diameter proxy of Definition~\ref{def:G} satisfies
\[
d(1/q)=2\pi\,G_0\,q^{-3}\bigl(1+O(q^{-1})\bigr),\qquad G_0=1.0264153852\ldots,
\]
and $G_0$ is determined by the same horn map (Table~\ref{tab:G}). More broadly, $\kappa(p/q)$ is the first correction to the Ford-circle description of $M$. Numerically, its dependence on $p/q$ is governed, after a transient, by the arithmetic of the modular inverse $p^{-1}\bmod q$, and it is singular at every rational~\cite{repo}. The renormalization picture explains that dependence, and $\kappa_0$ is its simplest instance.

\section{Setting and the index formula}\label{sec:index}

Fix $p/q$ in lowest terms, $\lambda_0=e^{2\pi ip/q}$, and write $f=f_{\lambda_0}$. Since $f$ has a single critical point, the parabolic fixed point $0$ has exactly one cycle of $q$ attracting petals, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fq}
f^q(z)=z+a\,z^{q+1}+O(z^{q+2}),\qquad a=a(p/q)\neq0 .
\end{equation}
The holomorphic index~\cite[\S12]{Milnor} of $0$ as a fixed point of $f^q$ is
\[
\iota_{p/q}:=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint\frac{dz}{z-f^q(z)}=\Res_{z=0}\frac{dz}{z-f^q(z)},
\]
and its r\'esidu it\'eratif~\cite{Ecalle,BE} is $\resit=\frac{q+1}{2}-\iota_{p/q}$.

\begin{theorem}[Index formula]\label{thm:index}
The multiplier $\rho_{p/q}(\varepsilon)$ of the $q$-cycle of $f_{\lambda_0e^\varepsilon}$ bifurcating from $0$ is holomorphic near $\varepsilon=0$, and
\[
\rho_{p/q}(\varepsilon)=1-q^2\varepsilon+q^3\Bigl(\iota_{p/q}-\frac12\Bigr)\varepsilon^2+O(\varepsilon^3).
\]
Equivalently, in \eqref{eq:R}, $r_0=1$, $r_1=-1$ and $\kappa(p/q)=(\iota_{p/q}-\frac12)/q$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose a disc $D$ around $0$ on whose boundary $z\ne f^q(z)$ and which contains no other fixed point of $f^q$. By \eqref{eq:fq} and Rouch\'e's theorem, for $|\varepsilon|$ small $f_\lambda^q$, $\lambda=\lambda_0e^\varepsilon$, has exactly $q+1$ fixed points in $D$, counted with multiplicity. One of them is $0$, with multiplier $\mu=\lambda^q=e^{q\varepsilon}\neq1$, so it is simple. For each proper divisor $d$ of $q$ the map $f^d$ has multiplier $\lambda_0^d\ne1$ at $0$, so $f_\lambda^d$ has no fixed point in $D$ other than $0$. The remaining $q$ points therefore have exact period $q$ and form a single cycle; call its multiplier $\rho(\varepsilon)$. The cycle, as an unordered set, depends holomorphically on $\lambda$, hence so does $\rho$, a symmetric function of the cycle points. The function $\rho$ is not identically $1$, since the cycle is attracting on the satellite, so for $\varepsilon\ne0$ small all $q+1$ fixed points are simple. Summing their indices $1/(1-m)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sum}
\frac1{1-e^{q\varepsilon}}+\frac q{1-\rho(\varepsilon)}=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{\partial D}\frac{dz}{z-f_\lambda^q(z)}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to0]{}\iota_{p/q},
\end{equation}
because the integrand converges uniformly on $\partial D$. The first term equals $-1/(q\varepsilon)+\frac12+O(\varepsilon)$ and is unbounded. Hence $\rho(0)=1$, and writing $1-\rho=A\varepsilon^m(1+O(\varepsilon))$ with $A\ne0$, boundedness of the sum forces $m=1$ and $A=q^2$. With $\rho=1-q^2\varepsilon+C\varepsilon^2+O(\varepsilon^3)$ we get $q/(1-\rho)=1/(q\varepsilon)+C/q^3+O(\varepsilon)$, and \eqref{eq:sum} yields $\frac12+C/q^3=\iota_{p/q}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:general}
The proof uses only that the parabolic point has multiplicity $q+1$. It therefore applies verbatim to the satellites of any hyperbolic component of $z^d+c$, whose parabolic points have a single cycle of petals. We checked this numerically for the period-two disc of $M$ and for $z^3+c$: the identity $r_2=(\iota-\frac12)/q$ holds to $10^{-7}$ at $p/q=2/7$ and $3/11$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Exact values]
Since $f^q$ has coefficients in $\Z[\lambda_0]$, the index is a rational function of $\lambda_0$ with rational coefficients, so $\iota_{p/q}\in\Q(\zeta_q)$ and $\iota_{p'/q}=\sigma(\iota_{p/q})$ for the automorphism $\sigma:\zeta_q\mapsto\zeta_q^{p'\bar p}$, $p\bar p\equiv1\pmod q$. For example $\iota_{1/2}=\frac18$, $\iota_{1/3}=\frac{100-8\sqrt3\,i}{441}$ and $\iota_{1/4}=\frac{1447-365\,i}{4624}$, so $\kappa(1/2)=-\frac3{16}$ and $\kappa(1/3)=\frac{-241-16\sqrt3\,i}{2646}$.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}\label{def:G}
Let $c_{\rm root}=c(\lambda_0)$, and let $c_{\rm ant}\in\partial W_{p/q}$ be the unique boundary point at which the $q$-cycle has multiplier $-1$. It is unique because the multiplier map $W_{p/q}\to\D$ is a conformal isomorphism in degree two. Set
\[
d(p/q)=|c_{\rm ant}-c_{\rm root}|,\qquad G(p/q)=\frac{d(p/q)}{2|c'(\lambda_0)|\,q^{-2}} .
\]
Since $c(\lambda_0e^{u/q^2})-c(\lambda_0)=c'(\lambda_0)\lambda_0u/q^2+O(q^{-4})$, we have $G(p/q)=\tfrac12|u_a|+O(q^{-2})$, where $R_{p/q}(u_a)=-1$. To first order $u_a=2$ and $G=1$; the deviation of $G$ from $1$ is governed at leading order by $\kappa$.
\end{definition}

\section{Normal forms and the rotation quotient}\label{sec:nf}

\begin{theorem}[Normal form]\label{thm:nf}
There is a unique formal conjugacy $H(z)=z+\sum_{k\ge2}H_kz^k$ with $H_{q+1}=H_{2q+1}=0$ such that $f\circ H=H\circ N$ up to order $2q+1$, where
\[
N(z)=\lambda_0z\bigl(1+b_1z^q+b_2z^{2q}\bigr).
\]
Put $\beta_i=\lambda_0b_i$ and let $(H^2)_k$ denote the coefficient of $z^k$ in $H(z)^2$. The coefficients are determined by the recursion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rec}
H_k(\lambda_0-\lambda_0^k)=[k>q+1]\,(k-q)\lambda_0^{k-q-1}\beta_1H_{k-q}-(H^2)_k\quad(2\le k\le 2q,\ k\ne q+1),
\end{equation}
together with $\beta_1=(H^2)_{q+1}$ and $\beta_2=(H^2)_{2q+1}$. Moreover
\[
a(p/q)=q\,b_1,\qquad q\,\iota_{p/q}=\frac{q^2-1}{2}+\frac{b_2}{b_1^2},\qquad
\kappa(p/q)=\frac12-\frac1{2q}-\frac1{2q^2}+\frac{b_2}{(qb_1)^2}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Comparing coefficients of $z^k$ in $\lambda_0H+H^2=\sum_mH_m N^m$ gives \eqref{eq:rec}. We use that $[N^m]_k$ is nonzero only for $k\in\{m,\,m+q,\,m+2q\}$, with values $\lambda_0^m$ and $m\lambda_0^{m-1}\beta_1$ in the first two cases. The factor $\lambda_0-\lambda_0^k$ vanishes exactly when $k\equiv1\pmod q$. At $k=q+1$ and $k=2q+1$ the equation fixes $\beta_1$, $\beta_2$ and leaves $H_{q+1}$, $H_{2q+1}$ free; we set them to zero. For the second part, write $N^j(z)=\lambda_0^jz(1+A_jz^q+B_jz^{2q}+\dots)$. Using $\lambda_0^q=1$ and $(N^j)^q=z^q(1+qA_jz^q+\dots)$,
\[
A_{j+1}=A_j+b_1,\qquad B_{j+1}=B_j+(q+1)b_1A_j+b_2,
\]
so $A_q=qb_1$ and $B_q=\frac{(q+1)q(q-1)}2b_1^2+qb_2$. The coefficient $a$ and the index are invariant under formal conjugacy tangent to the identity; for $z+Az^{q+1}+Bz^{2q+1}$ the index is $B/A^2$. This gives the formulas for $a$ and $\iota$, and the formula for $\kappa$ follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:index}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[$\kappa$ is an index]\label{cor:quotient}
Since $N$ commutes with $z\mapsto\lambda_0z$, the rotation quotient $\Phi_{p/q}(W):=N^q(W^{1/q})^q=W+q^2b_1W^2+\dots$ is a well-defined germ, and
\[
\kappa(p/q)=\iota(\Phi_{p/q})-\tfrac12=\tfrac12-\resit(\Phi_{p/q}).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
From $N^q(z)=z(1+A_qz^q+B_qz^{2q}+\dots)$ we get $\Phi(W)=W(1+A_qW+B_qW^2)^q+\dots$. Hence $\Phi=W+AW^2+BW^3+\dots$ with $A=qA_q=q^2b_1$ and $B=qB_q+\binom q2A_q^2$. Then $\iota(\Phi)=B/A^2=1-\frac1{2q}-\frac1{2q^2}+\frac{b_2}{(qb_1)^2}$, which is $\kappa+\frac12$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:nf}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Computation]
The recursion \eqref{eq:rec} costs $O(q^2)$ arithmetic operations, and it does not suffer cancellation. At $q=128$ and $q=301$ the computed $\kappa$ is unchanged in more than twenty digits when the working precision is raised from 60 to over 300 digits. Composing $f$ symbolically $q$ times instead costs $O(q^3)$ and loses about $q$ decimal digits to cancellation at $p=1$, where $|a(1/q)|\approx e^{2.3q}$. We used the latter, in ball arithmetic~\cite{Arb}, only as a certified cross-check (Appendix~\ref{app:num}).
\end{remark}

\section{The horn map of $z+z^2$}\label{sec:horn}

We recall the objects of parabolic implosion~\cite{Lavaurs,Sh,IS,LY} in the concrete form we compute with. In the coordinate $w=-1/z$ the map $f_0(z)=z+z^2$ becomes
\[
F(w)=\frac{w^2}{w-1}=w+1+\frac1{w-1}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Formal Fatou coordinate]\label{lem:fatou}
There is a unique formal series $\Phi(w)=w-\log w+\sum_{k\ge1}c_kw^{-k}$ with $\Phi\circ F=\Phi+1$. Its coefficients are rational:
\[c_1,c_2,\ldots=\tfrac12,\tfrac13,\tfrac{13}{36},\tfrac{113}{240},\tfrac{1187}{1800},\tfrac{877}{945},\ldots\] With $u=1/w$ they satisfy the triangular system
\[
\sum_{k\ge1}c_k\bigl[(u-u^2)^k-u^k\bigr]=-\sum_{m\ge2}\Bigl(1-\frac1m\Bigr)u^m .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
With $u=1/w$ we have $1/F=u-u^2$, $F-w-1=u/(1-u)$ and $\log F-\log w=-\log(1-u)$. Substituting gives the displayed identity. The coefficient of $u^m$ contains $c_{m-1}$ with factor $-(m-1)$ and otherwise only $c_k$ with $k<m-1$. The coefficient of $\log w$ is fixed by the order-$u$ term, and equals the r\'esidu it\'eratif $1$ of $f_0$.
\end{proof}

Let $\Phi_{\rm att}(w)=\lim_n\bigl(\Phi(F^n(w))-n\bigr)$ on the attracting petal, using the principal logarithm. Let $\Psi_{\rm rep}(Z)=\lim_nF^n\bigl(\Phi^{-1}(Z-n)\bigr)$ be the repelling Fatou parametrization, using the branch $\arg w\in(0,2\pi)$ on the left. The upper horn map $E=\Phi_{\rm att}\circ\Psi_{\rm rep}$ commutes with $Z\mapsto Z+1$ and, for $\operatorname{Im}Z$ large, has an expansion
\[
E(Z)=Z+a_0+\sum_{n\ge1}a_ne^{2\pi inZ}.
\]
Replacing $\Phi_{\rm att}$ or $\Psi_{\rm rep}$ by a translate changes $a_0$, and conjugates $E$ by $Z\mapsto Z+c$, which sends $a_n\mapsto a_ne^{2\pi inc}$. In $W=e^{2\pi iZ}$ the horn map is the germ $W\mapsto e^{2\pi ia_0}\PP(W)$, where
\[
\PP(W):=W\exp\Bigl(2\pi i\sum_{n\ge1}a_nW^n\Bigr)
\]
is the parabolic renormalization of $f_0$ normalized to multiplier one~\cite{LY,IS}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:horn-index}
$\displaystyle\iota(\PP)=\frac12+\frac{a_2}{2\pi i\,a_1^2}$. This quantity does not depend on the normalizations of the Fatou coordinates.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\PP(W)=W+AW^2+BW^3+\dots$ with $A=2\pi ia_1$ and $B=2\pi ia_2+\frac12(2\pi ia_1)^2$, and $\iota=B/A^2$. Under $a_n\mapsto a_ne^{2\pi inc}$ the ratio $a_2/a_1^2$ is unchanged; $a_0$ does not enter.
\end{proof}

\begin{observation}\label{obs:identity}
Computing $E$ on the line $\operatorname{Im}Z=\frac14$ at 60 and at 80 digits (Appendix~\ref{app:num}) gives $a_0=0$ to working precision ($|a_0|\approx10^{-58}$ at 60 digits) and
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{Re}a_1&=-0.05895043056440687000807026251551,\\
\operatorname{Im}a_1&=\phantom{-}0.010009865898273724511535250164253,\\
\operatorname{Re}a_2&=\phantom{-}0.0012858196663089284889517464706577,\\
\operatorname{Im}a_2&=\phantom{-}0.00011738770718282661923962731179377,
\end{align*}
with $|a_n|$ decreasing by a factor of about $55$ per step. Hence
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\iota(\PP)-\tfrac12\bigr)&=\phantom{-}0.023825887402200569000729189645402,\\
\operatorname{Im}\bigl(\iota(\PP)-\tfrac12\bigr)&=-0.052304659114003666316522486207571.
\end{align*}
The two precision settings agree in all 32 digits shown. This coincides with $c_0$ of Numerical result~\ref{obs:limit} to $3.1\cdot10^{-28}$; other stable fits differ from it by at most $5\cdot10^{-25}$. We therefore claim agreement to at least 24 digits, and 27 on the best fit. Already in double precision the agreement is $2.5\cdot10^{-11}$ at every height $\operatorname{Im}Z\in[\frac14,\frac12]$.
\end{observation}

\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:main}
The limit $\kappa_0=\lim_{q\to\infty}\kappa(1/q)$ exists and equals $\iota(\PP)-\frac12=\frac{a_2}{2\pi ia_1^2}$. Moreover
\[
\kappa(1/q)=\kappa_0-\frac{i}{2\pi q}+O(q^{-2}).
\]
\end{conjecture}

\section{Renormalization interpretation and further evidence}\label{sec:renorm}

\subsection{Heuristic}
For $\alpha$ near $0$, the near-parabolic renormalization $\mathcal R$ of Inou and Shishikura~\cite{IS} assigns to $f_\alpha(z)=e^{2\pi i\alpha}z+z^2$ a germ $\mathcal Rf_\alpha$ at $0$ with multiplier $e^{-2\pi i/\alpha}$. By parabolic implosion~\cite{Lavaurs,Sh}, as $\alpha\to0$ with $-1/\alpha\to\sigma\bmod\Z$, the germ $\mathcal Rf_\alpha$ tends to $e^{2\pi i\sigma}$ times the horn germ. For $\alpha=1/q$ the phase is $0$, and $\mathcal Rf_{1/q}$ is a parabolic germ with multiplier one. The rotation quotient $\Phi_{1/q}$ of Corollary~\ref{cor:quotient} is also a parabolic germ with multiplier one, obtained from $f^q$ by passing to the quotient by the $q$-fold rotation. If $\Phi_{1/q}$ and $\mathcal Rf_{1/q}$ are conformally conjugate, Corollary~\ref{cor:quotient} and continuity of $\mathcal R$ give Conjecture~\ref{conj:main}. The same reasoning leads to two further predictions, which we test next.

\subsection{Bounded $p$}\label{sec:boundedp}
Fix $p\ge2$ and $r$ with $\gcd(p,r)=1$, and let $q=pN+r\to\infty$. Then $-1/\alpha=-q/p\equiv-r/p\pmod1$, so $\mathcal Rf_{p/q}\to\mu\PP$ with $\mu=e^{-2\pi ir/p}$. This germ has multiplier a primitive $p$-th root of unity. Applying Corollary~\ref{cor:quotient} to it predicts
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundedp}
\lim_{N\to\infty}\kappa\Bigl(\frac p{pN+r}\Bigr)=\frac{\iota\bigl((\mu\PP)^p\bigr)-\frac12}{p},\qquad\mu=e^{-2\pi ir/p}.
\end{equation}
Table~\ref{tab:boundedp} compares \eqref{eq:boundedp} with bulb data for all nine classes with $p\le5$. The bulb values are normal-form values of $\kappa(p/q)$ for $N\le1024$, extrapolated in $1/q$. All nine agree to between $6\cdot10^{-9}$ and $2.6\cdot10^{-8}$, within the extrapolation uncertainty of about $10^{-7}$. The opposite phase convention, $\mu=e^{+2\pi ir/p}$, exchanges the classes $r$ and $p-r$ and fails. The first-order coefficients satisfy $c_1(p)\cdot2\pi p/(-i)=1.0008,\,1.0011,\,1.0014,\,1.0016$ for $p=2,\dots,5$, suggesting $c_1(p)=-i/(2\pi p)$ in general.

\subsection{The unfolding and the bulb size}\label{sec:unfolding}
With $\lambda=\lambda_0e^{u/q^2}$ one has $\alpha=\frac pq+\frac u{2\pi iq^2}$, so $e^{-2\pi i/\alpha}=\mu\,e^{u/p^2}+O(q^{-1})$. The picture therefore predicts that $R_{p/q}(u)$ converges to the multiplier $\rho_{p,r}(u)$ of the $p$-cycle of
\[
M_u(W)=\mu\,e^{u/p^2}\,\PP(W)
\]
that bifurcates from $W=0$.

For $p=1$ the Taylor coefficients of $\rho_{1,1}$ at $u=0$ agree with those of $R_{1/1025}$ through order eight, up to the finite-$q$ shift. For instance $r_3=-3.78\cdot10^{-4}-9.80\cdot10^{-4}i$ against $-3.62\cdot10^{-4}-9.72\cdot10^{-4}i$, and $r_8=3.14\cdot10^{-9}-4.56\cdot10^{-9}i$ against $3.13\cdot10^{-9}-4.50\cdot10^{-9}i$. Here $r_3$ depends on the unfolding and not only on the germ, so this checks that $u$ enters as the Lavaurs phase. The finite-$q$ shifts themselves are explained in Section~\ref{sec:kinematic}. Solving $\rho_{p,r}(u_a)=-1$ gives a prediction for $\lim G$. Table~\ref{tab:G} compares it with the bulb sizes for all ten classes with $p\le5$; the agreement is at most $9\cdot10^{-7}$.

\subsection{The first-order correction is kinematic}\label{sec:kinematic}
The unfolding above uses only the leading term $e^{-2\pi i/\alpha}\approx\mu e^{u/p^2}$. Exactly, the exponent is a M\"obius function of $u$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:kinematic}
Let $\lambda=e^{2\pi i\alpha}=\lambda_0e^{u/q^2}$ with $\lambda_0=e^{2\pi ip/q}$. Then $e^{-2\pi i/\alpha}=\mu\,e^{\tilde u/p^2}$ with $\mu=e^{-2\pi iq/p}$ and
\[
\tilde u=\frac{u}{1+u/(2\pi i\,pq)}=u+\frac{i\,u^2}{2\pi pq}+O\Bigl(\frac{u^3}{q^2}\Bigr).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\alpha=(2\pi ipq+u)/(2\pi iq^2)$, so $-1/\alpha+q/p=qu/\bigl(p(2\pi ipq+u)\bigr)$, and $2\pi i p^2$ times this is $\tilde u$.
\end{proof}

If the renormalized germ depends on $\alpha$ only through this multiplier, up to $O(q^{-2})$, then $R_{p/q}(u)=\rho_{p,r}(\tilde u(u))+O(q^{-2})$ coefficientwise. Comparing the $u^2$ and $u^3$ coefficients, with $\rho_{p,r}(u)=1-u+\kappa_{p,r}u^2+\rho_3u^3+\dots$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kinematic}
\kappa(p/q)=\kappa_{p,r}-\frac{i}{2\pi pq}+O(q^{-2}),\qquad r_3(p/q)=\rho_3+\frac{i\,\kappa_{p,r}}{\pi pq}+O(q^{-2}).
\end{equation}
The first identity is the observed coefficient $c_1(p)=-i/(2\pi p)$ of Conjecture~\ref{conj:main} and Section~\ref{sec:boundedp}. It involves no property of the germ, which explains why it is the same in degree three.

\begin{observation}\label{obs:kinematic}
Let $r_k(p/q)$ be the Taylor coefficients of the bulb (Cauchy integrals on $|u|=1.5$, double precision) and $\rho_k$ those of the renormalized family $M_u$ (Cauchy integrals on the same circle). For $2\le k\le8$, $(p,r)\in\{(1,1),(2,1),(3,1),(3,2)\}$ and $q=pN+r$ with $N=64,128,\dots,1024$, the residuals $|r_k(p/q)-[u^k]\rho_{p,r}(\tilde u)|$ decrease by a factor between $3.77$ and $4.28$ per doubling of $N$, in all $41$ cases where the residual exceeds $10^{-8}$; below that level the round-off of the Cauchy integrals takes over. Without the substitution $u\mapsto\tilde u$ the factor lies between $1.1$ and $2.0$. In degree three, with the cubic horn germ, Cauchy integrals on $|u|=1$ and $p=1$, the factor lies between $3.75$ and $4.06$ ($26$ cases).
\end{observation}

So through first order in $1/q$ the whole function $R_{p/q}$, not only $\kappa$, is the renormalized family composed with the exact Inou--Shishikura phase: the germ itself has no $O(1/q)$ correction. For $p=1$, $q=1025$, \eqref{eq:kinematic} predicts $r_3-\rho_3=1.62\cdot10^{-5}+0.74\cdot10^{-5}i$, while the values in Section~\ref{sec:unfolding} give $1.6\cdot10^{-5}+0.8\cdot10^{-5}i$.

\subsection{The hierarchy}\label{sec:hierarchy}
For $\alpha=[0;a_1,a_2,\ldots]$ the renormalization $\mathcal Rf_\alpha$ has multiplier $e^{-2\pi i/\alpha}$. Conjugating by $z\mapsto\bar z$ turns its rotation number into $1/\alpha\bmod1=[0;a_2,a_3,\ldots]$, so that $\mathcal R$ acts on continued fractions as the Gauss map~\cite{IS}. When the first $k$ partial quotients tend to infinity, the picture therefore predicts that each renormalization is close to $\PP$ applied to the complex conjugate of the previous one. Write $\bar G(W)=\overline{G(\bar W)}$, normalize germs to the form $v+v^2+O(v^3)$, and set
\[
G_1=\PP,\qquad G_{k+1}=\mathcal P(\bar G_k),\qquad \kappa_k=\iota(G_k)-\tfrac12 ,
\]
where $\mathcal P$ denotes the upper-horn-map construction of Section~\ref{sec:horn} applied to an arbitrary parabolic germ, so that $\kappa_k=a_2/(2\pi ia_1^2)$ for the horn map of $\bar G_{k-1}$.

The case $k=2$ can be tested against bulbs. By \eqref{eq:boundedp} with $r=1$, the limit of $\kappa\bigl([0;N,p]\bigr)$ as $N\to\infty$ is an index of $(e^{-2\pi i/p}\PP)^p$. We compute this index for $p\le256$ with the normal form of Theorem~\ref{thm:nf} generalized to arbitrary germs (an $O(p^3)$ recursion) and extrapolate in $1/p$. The result, $0.0112359330+0.0377814233\,i$, equals $\overline{\kappa_2}$ to $1.2\cdot10^{-8}$. The conjugate appears because $p^{-1}\bmod q\to0^-$ along this sequence, against $0^+$ for $p=1$. Without the conjugation in the definition of $G_2$ one obtains $0.014770-0.033911\,i$, which is excluded by the data.

For $k\ge3$ the germs $G_k$ are given by convergent series with small radius (about $2.6$ in the normalized coordinate $v$ at $k=2$), and the horn maps must be sampled at heights where the orbits stay well inside the disc. Table~\ref{tab:levels} lists $\kappa_1,\ldots,\kappa_7$ for two independent settings of height, Fourier truncation and precision. They agree to $2\cdot10^{-14}$ or better at every level.

\begin{observation}\label{obs:kstar}
The differences $\Delta_k=\kappa_k-\kappa_{k-1}$ decrease geometrically, $|\Delta_{k+1}/\Delta_k|\to0.0419$, with ratios $\Delta_{k+1}/\Delta_k\approx-0.01546\mp0.03894\,i$ alternating in sign of the imaginary part. This is the signature of an antilinear contraction $\Delta\mapsto\nu\bar\Delta$, the linearization of $G\mapsto\mathcal P(\bar G)$ being antilinear. Fitting $\Delta_{k+1}=M\Delta_k$ with $M$ real-linear on $\C\cong\R^2$ gives eigenvalues $\pm0.041899$ and the limit
\[
\kappa_*:=\lim_{k\to\infty}\kappa_k=0.011929183413-0.037455493752\,i,
\]
with the fits through levels $6$ and $7$ differing by less than $2\cdot10^{-12}$. Thus $\kappa_*+\frac12$ is the cubic coefficient of the normalized fixed point $G_*=\mathcal P(\bar G_*)$.
\end{observation}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:LY}
Without the conjugation, $G\mapsto\mathcal P(G)$ is the parabolic renormalization operator of Inou--Shishikura, whose fixed point $f_*$ was computed by Lanford and Yampolsky~\cite{LY}: $f_*(z)\approx z+z^2+(0.514-0.0346\,i)z^3$, with leading eigenvalue $\approx-0.017+0.040\,i$ of $D\mathcal P$ at $f_*$. Running our code without conjugation reproduces both: the cubic coefficients converge to $0.5142768-0.0346276\,i$ (extrapolated, $\pm10^{-7}$), with ratio of successive differences $\to-0.01764+0.04038\,i$. The bulbs, however, select the fixed point $G_*\ne f_*$ of the conjugated operator, whose cubic coefficient is $\kappa_*+\frac12=0.5119292-0.0374555\,i$.
\end{remark}

\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:hierarchy}
For every $k\ge1$,
\[
\lim_{N_k\to\infty}\cdots\lim_{N_1\to\infty}\kappa\bigl([0;N_1,\ldots,N_k]\bigr)=\begin{cases}\kappa_k,&k\text{ odd},\\ \overline{\kappa_k},&k\text{ even}.\end{cases}
\]
In particular $\kappa_*$ and $\overline{\kappa_*}$ are the limits of the first correction as the number of large partial quotients of $p/q$ grows, taken along odd and even $k$ respectively.
\end{conjecture}

The case $k=1$ is Numerical result~\ref{obs:identity}, and $k=2$ is the iterated limit discussed above. We also tested the diagonal sequences $[0;N,\ldots,N]$, computing $\kappa$ with the normal form in midpoint arithmetic at $128$ and $192$ bits (the two agree to $4\cdot10^{-35}$) and fitting polynomials in $1/N$. For $k=2$ and $16\le N\le160$ ($q\le25601$) the limit agrees with $\overline{\kappa_2}$ to $3\cdot10^{-10}$, so the diagonal and the iterated limit coincide. For $k=3$ and $6\le N\le40$ ($q\le64080$) the limit is $0.0119509937-0.0374804294\,i$. This agrees with $\kappa_3$ to $1.2\cdot10^{-6}$, and every fit with $N\ge10$ and $5$ or $6$ terms lies within $1.4\cdot10^{-5}$ of $\kappa_3$, whereas $|\kappa_3-\kappa_2|=7.8\cdot10^{-4}$ and $|\kappa_3-\overline{\kappa_3}|=7.5\cdot10^{-2}$. Thus the conjecture, including its parity rule, holds numerically for $k\le3$. Level $4$ ($|\kappa_4-\kappa_3|=3.3\cdot10^{-5}$) would require periods $q\approx N^4$ with $N\gtrsim30$.

\section{Degree three: a second universality class}\label{sec:degree3}

For $z^3+c$ the main component is parametrized by the multiplier $\lambda$ of the fixed point $z=(\lambda/3)^{1/2}$, with $c=z-z^3$. Its cusp at $\lambda=1$, where $z_*=1/\sqrt3$ and $c_*=2/(3\sqrt3)$, has the parabolic germ $w\mapsto w+\sqrt3\,w^2+w^3$. In $v=\sqrt3\,w$ this is
\[
g(v)=v+v^2+\tfrac13v^3,
\]
whose r\'esidu it\'eratif is $1-\frac13=\frac23$. Lemma~\ref{lem:fatou} generalizes with $F(w)=-1/g(-1/w)$ and $\Phi(w)=w-\frac23\log w+\sum c_kw^{-k}$, where $c_1,c_2,\ldots=\frac29,\frac5{54},\frac{14}{243},\frac{89}{2430},\ldots$. The horn map gives, at 50 and at 70 digits,
\[
\kappa_0^{(3)}:=\frac{a_2}{2\pi ia_1^2},\qquad
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Re}\kappa_0^{(3)}&=\phantom{-}0.143599278095291569488064104088,\\
\operatorname{Im}\kappa_0^{(3)}&=-0.030311647898502537611943110858.
\end{aligned}
\]
Table~\ref{tab:deg3} shows that $\kappa_3(1/q)+\frac i{2\pi q}$ converges to this value like $q^{-2}$. So the correction $-i/(2\pi q)$ is the same in degree three, while the constant is not. The bounded-$p$ predictions hold in degree three as well: $\kappa$ agrees to within $5\cdot10^{-6}$ for $(p,r)=(1,1),(2,1),(3,1),(3,2)$ at $q\approx512p$.

One subtlety concerns the bulb size. At the centre of a degree-$d$ satellite the critical point lies on the cycle, so the multiplier $\prod d\,z_j^{d-1}$ vanishes to order $d-1$. The multiplier map $W\to\D$ is proper, since the cycle becomes indifferent at $\partial W$, and each bounded hyperbolic component is a cell with a unique centre~\cite{MilnorHC}. So the multiplier map is a branched cover of degree $d-1$, and in degree three there are two points with multiplier $-1$. Both are predicted: at $p=3$, $r=1$ the horn map gives $G=1.196074$ and $1.227092$, against extrapolated bulb values $1.196075$ and $1.227091$.

\section{Arithmetic nature}\label{sec:pslq}

We searched with PSLQ~\cite{PSLQ}, at 30 digits and coefficient bound $1000$, for integer relations between each of $\operatorname{Re}\kappa_0$, $\operatorname{Im}\kappa_0$, $|\kappa_0|^2$, $\arg\kappa_0/\pi$, $\operatorname{Re}(2\pi i\kappa_0)$, $\operatorname{Im}(2\pi i\kappa_0)$, $|a_1|$ and $\arg a_1/\pi$, and every subset of at most three of $\{1,\pi,\pi^2,1/\pi,\log2,\zeta(3),G,\gamma\}$, where $G$ is Catalan's constant. No relation was found. This is consistent with the general expectation that invariants of parabolic germs, such as Écalle--Voronin moduli~\cite{Ecalle,Voronin}, are transcendental numbers without closed form. We regard $\kappa_0$ as a new constant of this kind, attached to the quadratic family and to the Mandelbrot set.

\section{Discussion}

The first-order theory of satellite bulbs is the Ford-circle picture, with radii $q^{-2}$ and Farey tangencies. The first correction, $\kappa(p/q)$, is the holomorphic index of a parabolic germ (Theorem~\ref{thm:index}) and equals the index of the rotation quotient minus $\frac12$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:quotient}). Numerically, it is controlled by near-parabolic renormalization. The constant $\kappa_0$ is the value at the fixed point of the simplest instance, $\alpha=1/q\to0$. Bounded $p$ corresponds to one renormalization step with phase $e^{-2\pi i r/p}$. General $p/q$ should correspond to iterating renormalization along the continued fraction of $p/q$; Section~\ref{sec:hierarchy} carries this out when all partial quotients are large, where the iteration contracts by a factor $0.042$ per step. Hyperbolicity of $\mathcal R$ would then explain why $\kappa(p/q)$ depends mainly on the last partial quotients, that is on $p^{-1}\bmod q$~\cite{repo}.

\subsection*{Open problems}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Prove Conjecture~\ref{conj:main}, for instance by establishing a conformal conjugacy between $\Phi_{1/q}$ and $\mathcal Rf_{1/q}$ together with continuity of $\mathcal R$ at integer $1/\alpha$.
\item Prove that the renormalized germ has no $O(1/q)$ correction beyond the phase of Proposition~\ref{prop:kinematic} (Numerical result~\ref{obs:kinematic}); by \eqref{eq:kinematic} this gives the universal term $-i/(2\pi pq)$.
\item Prove Conjecture~\ref{conj:hierarchy} (verified numerically for $k\le3$), and identify the fixed point $G_*$ of $G\mapsto\mathcal P(\bar G)$ and its spectrum.
\item Compute the generic limit function $\kappa(p/q)$, $p\to\infty$, by iterated renormalization with bounded partial quotients, including its values at quadratic irrationals as periodic points of $\mathcal R$.
\item Decide whether $\kappa_0$ can be expressed through Écalle--Voronin invariants of $z+z^2$ in closed form.
\end{enumerate}

\appendix
\section{Numerical methods and error control}\label{app:num}

\subsection*{A.1 Normal form}
Recursion \eqref{eq:rec} was run in \texttt{mpmath}~\cite{mpmath} at 50 working digits for $64\le q\le8192$ ($p=1$) and $q\le5124$ (bounded $p$). It was also implemented in Arb ball arithmetic~\cite{Arb}, which certifies every value. There the online convolution $(H^2)_k$ is evaluated in blocks: completed blocks are folded in by truncated series products, and the remainder is summed directly. The working precision is doubled until the certified radius of $\kappa$ is below $10^{-40}$. Ball radii are pessimistic, since they cannot see cancellation, but they remain small: for $p=1$ they are $1.6\cdot10^{-50}$ at $q=1024$ and below $2\cdot10^{-111}$ at $q=32768$. The two implementations agree to the 40 digits stored. The ball version is 60--110 times faster; $q=32768$ takes about two minutes.

\subsection*{A.2 Extrapolation}
Limits in $q$ come from least-squares fits in powers of $1/q$ at 40--60 digits. Each is reported only when it is stable, to the stated precision, under changes of the fit order and of the smallest $q$ used.

\subsection*{A.3 Horn maps}
The Fatou series were truncated at $K=40$ terms and evaluated at $|w|\ge800$. At that radius the truncation error is below $10^{-60}$, since $|c_k|$ grows roughly like $k!/(2\pi)^k$. We used $n=1000$--$1800$ repelling iterations and computed the Fourier coefficients from $64$--$80$ samples on $\operatorname{Im}Z=\frac14$, with aliasing below $10^{-58}$. Two independent settings agree to all digits reported. The coefficients $a_n$ extracted at height $h$ carry noise amplified by $e^{2\pi nh}$, so for the unfolding computations we used $n\le20$ coefficients computed at 60 digits.

\subsection*{A.4 Multiplier data}
The coefficients $r_k$ were also computed by Cauchy integrals of $R_{p/q}$ over a circle, continuing the $q$-cycle numerically. For $z^2+c$ the radius $|u|=2.5$ is admissible. For $z^3+c$ the function $R_{p/q}$ has a singularity inside $|u|<2.5$, and the radius must be at most $1.5$. The condition $r_1=-1$ from Theorem~\ref{thm:index} is a reliable diagnostic: it fails as $r_1=-1.032$ on the larger circle.

\subsection*{A.5 Reproducibility}
All code, data and tests are in the accompanying repository~\cite{repo}. The package \texttt{bulbford} contains \texttt{normal\_form} and \texttt{normal\_form\_ball}, \texttt{horn}, \texttt{germ}, \texttt{renorm} and \texttt{dynamics}; the cycle iteration in \texttt{dynamics} is compiled with Numba. The tables and figures of this paper are generated by \texttt{paper/tables.py} and \texttt{paper/figures.py}. The test suite checks Theorems~\ref{thm:index} and~\ref{thm:nf}, the horn-map value of $\kappa_0$ to 25 digits, and the bounded-$p$ and degree-three predictions.
