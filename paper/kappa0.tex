\title[Beyond the Ford-circle law]{Beyond the Ford-circle law:\\ parabolic renormalization and the shape\\ of Mandelbrot satellite bulbs}
\author{Lars Bendixen}
\date{September 2026}
\subjclass[2020]{Primary 37F46; Secondary 37F25, 37F10, 11A55}
\keywords{Mandelbrot set, satellite components, Ford circles, holomorphic index, r\'esidu it\'eratif, parabolic implosion, horn map, near-parabolic renormalization}

\begin{abstract}
To first order, the satellite component of the Mandelbrot set attached to the main cardioid at internal angle $p/q$ is a round disc of diameter proportional to $q^{-2}$; this is the Ford-circle law. We study the first correction. Expanding the multiplier of the bifurcating $q$-cycle in the bulb-scale parameter $u=q^2\varepsilon$, we prove that the first coefficient depending on $p/q$ is $\kappa(p/q)=(\iota_{p/q}-\frac12)/q$, where $\iota_{p/q}$ is the holomorphic index of the parabolic fixed point of $f^q$. We also prove that $\kappa(p/q)+\frac12$ is the index of the rotation quotient of $f^q$, and we give an exact $O(q^2)$ recursion for it. We then present numerical evidence, at up to $24$ digits, that the correction is governed by parabolic renormalization. The limit $\kappa_0=\lim\kappa(1/q)$ equals $\iota(\PP)-\frac12$, where $\PP$ is the parabolic renormalization of $z+z^2$. Along bounded $p$, the limits of $\kappa$ and of the bulb diameter are those of $\PP$ times the Inou--Shishikura phase. Numerically, the entire $O(1/q)$ correction is kinematic: it is produced by the exact phase, which is a M\"obius function of $u$. Along continued fractions with large partial quotients the constants form a hierarchy that converges geometrically, with ratio $0.042$, to a constant $\kappa_*$ attached to parabolic renormalization composed with complex conjugation; its fixed point differs from the one computed by Lanford and Yampolsky. At every rational $p/q$, the bulbs accumulating at the root of $W_{p/q}$ from above and from below are governed by the upper and lower parabolic renormalizations of $e^{2\pi ip/q}z+z^2$, so $\kappa$ has distinct one-sided limits at every rational; the period-two disc is governed by the same germ at its root. For $z^3+c$ the picture holds with the cubic cusp germ. We compute three new constants to 30 digits.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The Ford-circle law and its first correction}
Let $\lambda\in\D$ parametrize the main cardioid of the Mandelbrot set $M$ by the multiplier of the attracting fixed point, $c(\lambda)=\lambda/2-\lambda^2/4$. Equivalently we work with
\[
f_\lambda(z)=\lambda z+z^2 ,
\]
which is affinely conjugate to $z^2+c(\lambda)$. At each root of unity $\lambda_0=e^{2\pi i p/q}$, $\gcd(p,q)=1$, a satellite hyperbolic component $W_{p/q}$ of period $q$ is attached to the cardioid~\cite{DH}. Its size is of order $q^{-2}$~\cite{GM}. More precisely, to first order in $1/q$ it is a round disc of diameter $2|c'(\lambda_0)|q^{-2}=2\sin(\pi p/q)\,q^{-2}$~\cite{FM}. Up to the factor $\sin(\pi p/q)$ these are the radii of the Ford circles~\cite{Ford}, and the satellites are arranged in the Farey order~\cite{Devaney}; this is the leading-order content of the familiar analogy between $M$ and the Farey tessellation.

This paper studies the \emph{first correction} to that law, which to our knowledge has not been investigated before. Put $\lambda=\lambda_0e^{\varepsilon}$ and let $\rho_{p/q}(\varepsilon)$ be the multiplier of the $q$-cycle of $f_\lambda$ that bifurcates from the fixed point $0$. In the bulb-scale variable $u=q^2\varepsilon$ write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R}
R_{p/q}(u):=\rho_{p/q}(u/q^2)=\sum_{k\ge0} r_k(p/q)\,u^k .
\end{equation}
Up to the conformal change of variable $u\mapsto c(\lambda_0e^{u/q^2})$, the component $W_{p/q}$ is the set $\{|R_{p/q}(u)|<1\}$. We show that $r_0=1$ and $r_1=-1$ for every $p/q$, so that to first order $W_{p/q}$ is the disc $|u-1|<1$: this is the Ford law. The first coefficient that depends on $p/q$ is
\[
\kappa(p/q):=r_2(p/q),
\]
and it governs the leading deviation of $W_{p/q}$ from a round disc. When $\lambda=e^{2\pi i\alpha}$ we also write $\fa{\alpha}$ for $f_\lambda$; in particular $f_1(z)=z+z^2$.

\subsection{Exact results}
Let $f=f_{\lambda_0}$, so that $f^q(z)=z+a\,z^{q+1}+O(z^{q+2})$ with $a\ne0$, and let $\iota_{p/q}=\Res_{z=0}dz/(z-f^q(z))$ be the holomorphic index of $0$ as a fixed point of $f^q$.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Index formula} (Theorem~\ref{thm:index}). $\rho_{p/q}(\varepsilon)=1-q^2\varepsilon+q^3(\iota_{p/q}-\frac12)\varepsilon^2+O(\varepsilon^3)$, that is, $\kappa(p/q)=(\iota_{p/q}-\frac12)/q$. In particular $\kappa(p/q)\in\Q(\zeta_q)$, and the values $\kappa(p'/q)$, $\gcd(p',q)=1$, form a single Galois orbit. The index formula holds for the satellites of every hyperbolic component of $z^d+c$.
\item \textbf{Normal form} (Theorem~\ref{thm:nf}, Corollary~\ref{cor:quotient}). If $\NF(z)=\lambda_0z(1+b_1z^q+b_2z^{2q})$ is the resonant normal form of $f$, then $q\,\iota_{p/q}=\frac{q^2-1}2+b_2/b_1^2$ and $\kappa(p/q)=\iota(Q_{p/q})-\frac12$, where $Q_{p/q}(W)=\NF^q(W^{1/q})^q$ is the $q$-fold rotation quotient of the normal form of $f^q$. The coefficients $b_1,b_2$ come from an explicit recursion with $O(q^2)$ operations.
\item \textbf{Horn-map indices} (Lemma~\ref{thm:horn-index}). If $E(Z)=Z+a_0+\sum_{n\ge1}a_ne^{2\pi inZ}$ is an upper horn map and $\mathcal P(W)=W\exp(2\pi i\sum_na_nW^n)$ the corresponding parabolic germ, then $\iota(\mathcal P)-\frac12=a_2/(2\pi ia_1^2)$.
\item \textbf{Kinematics} (Proposition~\ref{prop:kinematic}). If $\lambda=e^{2\pi i\alpha}=\lambda_0e^{u/q^2}$, then exactly $e^{-2\pi i/\alpha}=e^{-2\pi iq/p}e^{\tilde u/p^2}$, where $\tilde u=u/(1+\frac{u}{2\pi ipq})$ is a M\"obius function of $u$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Numerical results and conjectures}
The rest of the paper supports one picture: up to $O(q^{-2})$, the bulb $W_{p/q}$ is described by a near-parabolic renormalization of $f_{\lambda_0}$ in the sense of Inou and Shishikura~\cite{IS}, composed with the exact phase of Proposition~\ref{prop:kinematic} (Conjecture~\ref{conj:unfolding}). The main consequences, each tested against the bulbs, are listed below. Table~\ref{tab:constants} collects the constants.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{The constant $\kappa_0$} (\S\ref{sec:horn}). $\kappa(1/q)\to\kappa_0=\iota(\PP)-\frac12$, where $\PP$ is the parabolic renormalization of $z+z^2$. The extrapolation of values certified up to $q=32768$ agrees with the horn map to at least $24$ digits ($3\cdot10^{-28}$ on the best fit).
\item \emph{Bounded $p$ and the bulb size} (\S\ref{sec:renorm}). Along $q=pN+r$ the limits of $\kappa$ and of the bulb diameter are those of the family $e^{-2\pi ir/p}e^{u/p^2}\PP$. In all classes with $p\le5$ they agree within the extrapolation uncertainties, which are about $10^{-7}$ for $\kappa$ and $10^{-6}$ for the diameter.
\item \emph{Kinematics} (\S\ref{sec:kinematic}). Numerically, $R_{p/q}(u)=\rho_{p,r}(\tilde u(u))+O(q^{-2})$ coefficientwise. This accounts for the universal term $-i/(2\pi pq)$ in $\kappa$ and for the whole $O(1/q)$ part of $R_{p/q}$.
\item \emph{The hierarchy} (\S\ref{sec:hierarchy}). Iterating along continued fractions with large partial quotients gives constants $\kappa_k$ that contract by the factor $0.042$ per level towards $\kappa_*$. The levels $k\le3$ are confirmed on bulbs, the third to about $10^{-5}$. The fixed point differs from that of Lanford and Yampolsky~\cite{LY}, whose computations our code reproduces.
\item \emph{Limits at the roots of satellites} (\S\ref{sec:component}). As the internal angle tends to a rational $p/q$ with the last partial quotient tending to infinity, $\kappa$ tends to the index of the upper (from above) or lower (from below) parabolic renormalization of $e^{2\pi ip/q}z+z^2$, and the whole bounded-tail theory carries over. For $q\ge3$ the two one-sided limits are not conjugate; eleven cases at $1/3$, $1/4$, $2/5$ and $3/7$ agree to $6\cdot10^{-11}$. The satellites of the period-two disc near its root follow the same germ ($q=2$), with its own constant $\kappa_{1/2}$. We conjecture that in general only the root germ matters.
\item \emph{Degree three} (\S\ref{sec:degree3}). For $z^3+c$ the same predictions hold numerically with the cusp germ $v+v^2+\frac13v^3$; the bulbs now have two antipodes (Proposition~\ref{prop:antipodes}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Related work}
The $q^{-2}$ law is due to Guckenheimer and McGehee~\cite{GM}, and the leading constant $2\sin(\pi p/q)$ was derived by Fowler and McGuinness~\cite{FM}. Parabolic implosion and horn maps go back to Douady, Lavaurs and Shishikura~\cite{Lavaurs,Sh}. Near-parabolic renormalization and its invariant class are due to Inou and Shishikura~\cite{IS}, and Lanford and Yampolsky~\cite{LY} studied the fixed point of parabolic renormalization numerically. Near-parabolic renormalization for unicritical maps of higher degree was developed by Ch\'eritat~\cite{Cheritat} and, for local degree three, by Yang~\cite{Yang}. Buff and Ch\'eritat~\cite{BuffCheritat} used parabolic implosion to control the size of quadratic Siegel discs in terms of the Brjuno function, which is the closest precedent we know for quantitative asymptotics of this kind. The holomorphic index and the r\'esidu it\'eratif are classical~\cite{Milnor,Ecalle}, and Buff and Epstein~\cite{BE} proved an inequality of Pommerenke--Levin--Yoccoz type for parabolic cycles, which by Theorem~\ref{thm:index} constrains $\kappa$; we do not pursue this here. The Ford-circle analogy is usually stated only to leading order~\cite{Devaney}. We are not aware of previous work on the corrections, and we have not found Theorem~\ref{thm:index} in the literature, although it follows directly from the holomorphic fixed-point formula.

\section{The index formula}\label{sec:index}

Fix $p/q$ in lowest terms, $\lambda_0=e^{2\pi ip/q}$, and write $f=f_{\lambda_0}$. Since every cycle of petals attracts a critical point~\cite[\S10]{Milnor} and $f$ has only one, the parabolic fixed point $0$ has exactly one cycle of $q$ attracting petals, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fq}
f^q(z)=z+a\,z^{q+1}+O(z^{q+2}),\qquad a=a(p/q)\neq0 .
\end{equation}
The holomorphic index~\cite[\S12]{Milnor} of $0$ as a fixed point of $f^q$ is
\[
\iota_{p/q}:=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint\frac{dz}{z-f^q(z)}=\Res_{z=0}\frac{dz}{z-f^q(z)},
\]
and its r\'esidu it\'eratif~\cite{Ecalle,BE} is $\resit(f^q)=\frac{q+1}{2}-\iota_{p/q}$. In general, for a fixed point of multiplier one and multiplicity $m+1$ we set $\resit=\frac{m+1}2-\iota$.

\begin{theorem}[Index formula]\label{thm:index}
The multiplier $\rho_{p/q}(\varepsilon)$ of the $q$-cycle of $f_{\lambda_0e^\varepsilon}$ that bifurcates from $0$ is holomorphic near $\varepsilon=0$, and
\[
\rho_{p/q}(\varepsilon)=1-q^2\varepsilon+q^3\Bigl(\iota_{p/q}-\frac12\Bigr)\varepsilon^2+O(\varepsilon^3).
\]
Equivalently, in \eqref{eq:R}, $r_0=1$, $r_1=-1$ and $\kappa(p/q)=(\iota_{p/q}-\frac12)/q$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose a disc $D$ around $0$ on whose boundary $z\ne f^q(z)$, and so small that $0$ is the only fixed point of $f^\ell$ in $\overline D$ for every $\ell\le q$; by continuity the second property persists for $f_\lambda^\ell$, $\ell<q$, when $\lambda$ is close to $\lambda_0$. By \eqref{eq:fq} and Rouch\'e's theorem, for $|\varepsilon|$ small the map $f_\lambda^q$, $\lambda=\lambda_0e^\varepsilon$, has exactly $q+1$ fixed points in $D$, counted with multiplicity. One of them is $0$, with multiplier $\lambda^q=e^{q\varepsilon}\neq1$, so it is simple. The remaining $q$ points therefore have exact period $q$. They are permuted by $f_\lambda$, since $f_\lambda$ maps fixed points of $f_\lambda^q$ near $0$ to fixed points of $f_\lambda^q$ near $0$, and they form a single cycle because $f_\lambda$ is close to the rotation by $\lambda_0$ near $0$; call its multiplier $\rho(\varepsilon)=\prod_j(\lambda+2z_j)$. The power sums $\sum_jz_j^k=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{\partial D}z^k\frac{1-(f_\lambda^q)'(z)}{z-f_\lambda^q(z)}\,dz$, $k\ge1$, are holomorphic in $\lambda$, and hence so is $\rho$, a symmetric polynomial in the $z_j$. The function $\rho$ is not identically $1$, since the cycle is attracting on the satellite, so for $\varepsilon\ne0$ small all $q+1$ fixed points are simple. Summing their indices $1/(1-m_j)$, where $m_j$ are the multipliers, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sum}
\frac1{1-e^{q\varepsilon}}+\frac q{1-\rho(\varepsilon)}=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{\partial D}\frac{dz}{z-f_\lambda^q(z)}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to0]{}\iota_{p/q},
\end{equation}
because the integrand converges uniformly on $\partial D$. The first term equals $-1/(q\varepsilon)+\frac12+O(\varepsilon)$ and is unbounded. Hence $\rho(0)=1$. Writing $1-\rho=A\varepsilon^n(1+O(\varepsilon))$ with $A\ne0$, boundedness of the sum forces $n=1$ and $A=q^2$. With $\rho=1-q^2\varepsilon+C\varepsilon^2+O(\varepsilon^3)$ we get $q/(1-\rho)=1/(q\varepsilon)+C/q^3+O(\varepsilon)$, and \eqref{eq:sum} yields $\frac12+C/q^3=\iota_{p/q}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Other components]\label{rem:general}
The proof uses only that the parabolic point has multiplicity $q+1$. It therefore applies verbatim to the satellites of any hyperbolic component of $z^d+c$, whose parabolic cycles have a single cycle of petals, with $\lambda$ the multiplier of the attracting cycle of that component (so that the first return map has multiplier exactly $\lambda$ at the cycle point) and $f^q$ replaced by the $q$-th iterate of the first return map. The arithmetic statements of Remark~\ref{rem:exact} use that $f_{\lambda_0}$ has coefficients in $\Z[\lambda_0]$ and do not extend verbatim. We checked numerically, for the period-two disc and for $z^3+c$, that $r_2=(\iota-\frac12)/q$ holds to $10^{-7}$ at $p/q=2/7$ and $3/11$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Exact values]\label{rem:exact}
Since $f^q$ has coefficients in $\Z[\lambda_0]$, the index is a rational function of $\lambda_0$ with rational coefficients. Hence $\iota_{p/q}\in\Q(\zeta_q)$ and $\iota_{p'/q}=\sigma(\iota_{p/q})$ for the automorphism $\sigma:\zeta_q\mapsto\zeta_q^{p'\bar p}$, where $p\bar p\equiv1\pmod q$. For example $\iota_{1/2}=\frac18$, $\iota_{1/3}=\frac{100-8\sqrt3\,i}{441}$ and $\iota_{1/4}=\frac{1447-365\,i}{4624}$, so that $\kappa(1/2)=-\frac3{16}$ and $\kappa(1/3)=\frac{-241-16\sqrt3\,i}{2646}$.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}\label{def:G}
Let $c_{\rm root}=c(\lambda_0)$, and let $c_{\rm ant}\in\partial W_{p/q}$ be the boundary point at which the $q$-cycle has multiplier $-1$. It is unique because in degree two the multiplier map extends to a homeomorphism $\overline{W}_{p/q}\to\overline\D$~\cite{DH}. Set
\[
\delta(p/q)=|c_{\rm ant}-c_{\rm root}|,\qquad G(p/q)=\frac{\delta(p/q)}{2|c'(\lambda_0)|\,q^{-2}} .
\]
Since $c''\equiv-\frac12$ and $|c'(\lambda_0)|=\frac12|1-\lambda_0|=\sin(\pi p/q)$, we have $c(\lambda_0e^{u/q^2})-c(\lambda_0)=c'(\lambda_0)\lambda_0\frac u{q^2}\bigl(1+O(\frac{|u|}{q^2\sin(\pi p/q)})\bigr)$, and therefore $G(p/q)=\tfrac12|u_a|\bigl(1+O(\frac1{q^2\sin(\pi p/q)})\bigr)$, where $R_{p/q}(u_a)=-1$. For bounded $p$ the error is $O(1/(pq))$. To first order $u_a=2$ and $G=1$; the deviation of $G$ from $1$ depends on all the $r_k$, of which $\kappa$ is the first.
\end{definition}

\section{Normal forms and the rotation quotient}\label{sec:nf}

\begin{theorem}[Normal form]\label{thm:nf}
There is a unique polynomial $H(z)=z+\sum_{k=2}^{2q}H_kz^k$ with $H_{q+1}=0$ such that $f\circ H=H\circ\NF$ up to order $2q+1$, where
\[
\NF(z)=\lambda_0z\bigl(1+b_1z^q+b_2z^{2q}\bigr).
\]
Put $\hat b_i=\lambda_0b_i$ and let $(H^2)_k$ denote the coefficient of $z^k$ in $H(z)^2$. The coefficients are determined by the recursion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rec}
H_k(\lambda_0-\lambda_0^k)=[k>q+1]\,(k-q)\lambda_0^{k-q-1}\hat b_1H_{k-q}-(H^2)_k\quad(2\le k\le 2q,\ k\ne q+1),
\end{equation}
together with $\hat b_1=(H^2)_{q+1}$ and $\hat b_2=(H^2)_{2q+1}$. Moreover
\[
a(p/q)=q\,b_1,\qquad q\,\iota_{p/q}=\frac{q^2-1}{2}+\frac{b_2}{b_1^2},\qquad
\kappa(p/q)=\frac12-\frac1{2q}-\frac1{2q^2}+\frac{b_2}{(qb_1)^2}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Comparing coefficients of $z^k$ in $\lambda_0H+H^2=\sum_mH_m\NF^m$ gives \eqref{eq:rec}. Here we use that $[\NF^m]_k$ is nonzero only for $k\in\{m,\,m+q,\,m+2q,\dots\}$, with values $\lambda_0^m$ and $m\lambda_0^{m-1}\hat b_1$ in the first two cases. The factor $\lambda_0-\lambda_0^k$ vanishes exactly when $k\equiv1\pmod q$. At $k=q+1$ and $k=2q+1$ the equation fixes $\hat b_1$ and $\hat b_2$ and leaves $H_{q+1}$ free; we set it to zero. For the second part, write $\NF^j(z)=\lambda_0^jz(1+A_jz^q+B_jz^{2q}+\dots)$. Using $\lambda_0^q=1$ and $(\NF^j)^q=z^q(1+qA_jz^q+\dots)$,
\[
A_{j+1}=A_j+b_1,\qquad B_{j+1}=B_j+(q+1)b_1A_j+b_2,
\]
so $A_q=qb_1$ and $B_q=\frac{(q+1)q(q-1)}2b_1^2+qb_2$. The coefficient $a$ and the index are invariant under formal conjugacy tangent to the identity, and for $z+Az^{q+1}+Bz^{2q+1}$ the index is $B/A^2$. This gives the formulas for $a$ and $\iota$, and the formula for $\kappa$ follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:index}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[$\kappa$ is an index]\label{cor:quotient}
Since $\NF$ commutes with $z\mapsto\lambda_0z$, the rotation quotient $Q_{p/q}(W):=\NF^q(W^{1/q})^q=W+q^2b_1W^2+\dots$ is a well-defined germ, and
\[
\kappa(p/q)=\iota(Q_{p/q})-\tfrac12=\tfrac12-\resit(Q_{p/q}).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
From $\NF^q(z)=z(1+A_qz^q+B_qz^{2q}+\dots)$ we get $Q_{p/q}(W)=W(1+A_qW+B_qW^2)^q+\dots$. Hence $Q_{p/q}=W+AW^2+BW^3+\dots$ with $A=qA_q=q^2b_1$ and $B=qB_q+\binom q2A_q^2$. Then $\iota(Q_{p/q})=B/A^2=1-\frac1{2q}-\frac1{2q^2}+\frac{b_2}{(qb_1)^2}$, which is $\kappa+\frac12$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:nf}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Relation with the r\'esidu it\'eratif]\label{rem:resit}
Only the $3$-jet of $Q_{p/q}$ is determined by $f$; the higher coefficients depend on the truncation of the normal form, and all that enters $\iota(Q_{p/q})$ is the $3$-jet. In terms of the r\'esidu it\'eratif, Theorem~\ref{thm:index} reads $\kappa(p/q)=\frac12-\resit(f^q)/q$. Corollary~\ref{cor:quotient} is equivalent to it: with $Q_1(W)=\NF(W^{1/q})^q$ one has $Q_{p/q}=Q_1^q$, a direct computation from Theorem~\ref{thm:nf} gives $\resit(Q_1)=\frac{q+1}{2q}-\frac{b_2}{qb_1^2}=\resit(f^q)$, and $\resit(g^q)=\resit(g)/q$ for any germ $g(W)=W+AW^2+BW^3+\dots$ with $A\ne0$. The content of Theorem~\ref{thm:nf} is the recursion.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Computation]\label{rem:computation}
The recursion \eqref{eq:rec} costs $O(q^2)$ arithmetic operations. It is numerically stable: at $q=128$ and $q=301$ the values of $\kappa(1/q)$ computed at $60$ and at over $300$ working digits agree to more than twenty digits, and for generic $p$ with $q$ up to $6.4\cdot10^4$ two runs at $128$ and $192$ bits agree to $4\cdot10^{-35}$. By contrast, composing $f$ symbolically $q$ times costs $O(q^3)$ and suffers severe cancellation: the coefficients of $f^q$ are huge (for instance $|a(1/q)|$ grows like $(q/2\pi)^{q+o(q)}$, and $|a(1/256)|\approx10^{414}$), and the index computed that way needed about $1.5q$ working digits. With a blocked online convolution the recursion runs in ball arithmetic~\cite{Arb}, which certifies $\kappa(1/q)$ for $q\le32768$ (Appendix~\ref{app:num}).
\end{remark}

\begin{observation}\label{obs:limit}
Least-squares fits $\kappa(1/q)=\sum_{j\le J}\gamma_jq^{-j}$ over $q\ge q_{\min}$ are stable under changes of $(q_{\min},J)$. The best overdetermined fit, $(q_{\min},J)=(256,10)$ on thirteen values with residual $3.6\cdot10^{-29}$, gives
\[
\gamma_0=0.023825887402200569000729189339\ldots-0.052304659114003666316522486209\ldots\,i ,
\]
the first-order coefficient $\gamma_1=-i/(2\pi)$ to within $2\cdot10^{-23}$, and $\gamma_2=0.402776138694+2.902726170410\,i$. The fits with $(q_{\min},J)=(128,10)$, $(256,9)$ and $(512,9)$ give $\gamma_0$ within $3\cdot10^{-25}$ of this value. The ratios $|\gamma_{j+1}/\gamma_j|$ ($2.8$, $18.4$, $6.3$, $6.9,\dots,8.2$) drift slowly upward, so the expansion may be asymptotic rather than convergent; we use the fits only as extrapolations.
\end{observation}

\subsection{Fatou coordinates and the horn map}
We recall the objects of parabolic implosion~\cite{Lavaurs,Sh,IS,LY} in the form in which we compute them. In the coordinate $w=-1/z$ the map $f_1(z)=z+z^2$ becomes
\[
F(w)=\frac{w^2}{w-1}=w+1+\frac1{w-1}.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Formal Fatou coordinate]\label{lem:fatou}
There is a unique formal series $\Phi(w)=w-\log w+\sum_{k\ge1}c_kw^{-k}$ without constant term such that $\Phi\circ F=\Phi+1$. Its coefficients are rational,
\[c_1,c_2,\ldots=\tfrac12,\tfrac13,\tfrac{13}{36},\tfrac{113}{240},\tfrac{1187}{1800},\tfrac{877}{945},\ldots,\]
and with $t=1/w$ they satisfy the triangular system
\[
\sum_{k\ge1}c_k\bigl[(t-t^2)^k-t^k\bigr]=-\sum_{m\ge2}\Bigl(1-\frac1m\Bigr)t^m .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
With $t=1/w$ we have $1/F=t-t^2$, $F-w-1=t/(1-t)$ and $\log F-\log w=-\log(1-t)$. Substituting gives the displayed identity. The coefficient of $t^m$ contains $c_{m-1}$ with the factor $-(m-1)$ and otherwise only $c_k$ with $k<m-1$. The coefficient of $\log w$ is fixed by the term of order $t$; it equals the r\'esidu it\'eratif $1$ of $f_1$.
\end{proof}

Let $\Phi_{\rm att}(w)=\lim_n\bigl(\Phi(F^n(w))-n\bigr)$ on the attracting petal, with the principal logarithm, and let $\Psi_{\rm rep}(Z)=\lim_nF^n\bigl(\Phi^{-1}(Z-n)\bigr)$ be the repelling Fatou parametrization, with the branch $\arg w\in(0,2\pi)$. The upper horn map $E=\Phi_{\rm att}\circ\Psi_{\rm rep}$ commutes with $Z\mapsto Z+1$ and, for $\operatorname{Im}Z$ large, has an expansion
\[
E(Z)=Z+a_0+\sum_{n\ge1}a_ne^{2\pi inZ}.
\]
For $\operatorname{Im}Z>0$ the two branches of the logarithm agree, so both Fatou coordinates have the same asymptotic expansion there and $a_0=0$ with these normalizations. Translating both $\Phi_{\rm att}$ and $\Psi_{\rm rep}$ by $\tau$ conjugates $E$ by $Z\mapsto Z+\tau$, which sends $a_n\mapsto a_ne^{2\pi in\tau}$; translating only one of them changes $a_0$. In $W=e^{2\pi iZ}$ the horn map is the germ $W\mapsto e^{2\pi ia_0}\PP(W)$, where
\[
\PP(W):=W\exp\Bigl(2\pi i\sum_{n\ge1}a_nW^n\Bigr)
\]
is the parabolic renormalization of $f_1$, normalized to multiplier one~\cite{IS,LY}. More generally, let $g(v)=v+v^2+O(v^3)$ be holomorphic near $0$. The same construction in $w=-1/v$, with $\Phi(w)=w-\resit(g)\log w+\sum c_kw^{-k}$ and the same branches, gives an upper horn map and a germ $W\exp(2\pi i\sum_na_nW^n)$. Conjugating it by $W\mapsto2\pi ia_1W$ we normalize it to the form $v+v^2+O(v^3)$, and we call the result $\mathcal P(g)$. Thus $\PP$ and $\mathcal P(f_1)$ differ only by this linear conjugacy.

\begin{lemma}\label{thm:horn-index}
If $a_1\ne0$, then $\displaystyle\iota(\PP)=\frac12+\frac{a_2}{2\pi i\,a_1^2}$. This quantity does not depend on the normalizations of the Fatou coordinates, and the same formula holds for $\mathcal P(g)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\PP(W)=W+AW^2+BW^3+\dots$ with $A=2\pi ia_1$ and $B=2\pi ia_2+\frac12(2\pi ia_1)^2$, and $\iota=B/A^2$. Under $a_n\mapsto a_ne^{2\pi in\tau}$ the ratio $a_2/a_1^2$ is unchanged, $a_0$ does not enter, and $\iota$ is invariant under conjugacy.
\end{proof}

\begin{observation}\label{obs:identity}
Computing $E$ on the line $\operatorname{Im}Z=\frac14$ at 60 and at 80 digits (Appendix~\ref{app:num}) gives $|a_0|<10^{-58}$, as it must with these branches, and
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{Re}a_1&=-0.05895043056440687000807026251551,\\
\operatorname{Im}a_1&=\phantom{-}0.010009865898273724511535250164253,\\
\operatorname{Re}a_2&=\phantom{-}0.0012858196663089284889517464706577,\\
\operatorname{Im}a_2&=\phantom{-}0.00011738770718282661923962731179377,
\end{align*}
with $|a_n/a_{n+1}|$ increasing to about $55.6$. Hence
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{Re}\bigl(\iota(\PP)-\tfrac12\bigr)&=\phantom{-}0.023825887402200569000729189645402,\\
\operatorname{Im}\bigl(\iota(\PP)-\tfrac12\bigr)&=-0.052304659114003666316522486207571.
\end{align*}
The two precision settings agree in all digits shown. This value coincides with $\gamma_0$ of Numerical result~\ref{obs:limit} to $3.1\cdot10^{-28}$, and the other stable fits differ from it by at most $3\cdot10^{-25}$. We therefore claim agreement to at least $24$ digits.
\end{observation}

\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:main}
The limit $\kappa_0=\lim_{q\to\infty}\kappa(1/q)$ exists and equals $\iota(\PP)-\frac12=\frac{a_2}{2\pi ia_1^2}$. Moreover $\kappa(1/q)=\kappa_0-\frac{i}{2\pi q}+O(q^{-2})$.
\end{conjecture}

\section{The renormalization principle}\label{sec:renorm}

\subsection{Heuristic}
For $\alpha$ near $0$, the near-parabolic renormalization $\mathcal R$ of Inou and Shishikura~\cite{IS} assigns to $\fa{\alpha}$ a germ $\mathcal R\fa{\alpha}$ at $0$ with multiplier $e^{-2\pi i/\alpha}$. By parabolic implosion~\cite{Lavaurs,Sh}, as $\alpha\to0$ with $-1/\alpha\to\sigma\bmod\Z$, the germ $\mathcal R\fa{\alpha}$ tends to $e^{2\pi i\sigma}$ times the horn germ. For $\alpha=1/q$ the phase is $0$, and $\mathcal R\fa{1/q}$ is a parabolic germ with multiplier one. The rotation quotient $Q_{1/q}$ of Corollary~\ref{cor:quotient} is also a germ with multiplier one, and its $3$-jet, which determines $\kappa(1/q)$, is determined by $f^q$ (Remark~\ref{rem:resit}). If the $3$-jets of $Q_{1/q}$ and of $\mathcal R\fa{1/q}$ are formally conjugate, then continuity of $\mathcal R$ gives the first statement of Conjecture~\ref{conj:main}.

A proof along these lines needs two steps that we have not carried out: (a) the identification of $\iota(Q_{1/q})$ with the index of $\mathcal R\fa{1/q}$, that is, of the parabolic flower of $f^q$ at $0$ with the Fatou coordinates used to define $\mathcal R$; and (b) the convergence $\mathcal R\fa{1/q}\to\PP$ uniformly on a fixed disc, which should follow from parabolic implosion and gives convergence of the index by the residue formula. The rate $O(q^{-2})$ in Conjecture~\ref{conj:main} requires more, namely the first-order statement of Conjecture~\ref{conj:unfolding} below. The same reasoning applies to the whole unfolding, and there the phase can be computed exactly.

\begin{proposition}[Kinematics]\label{prop:kinematic}
Let $\lambda=e^{2\pi i\alpha}=\lambda_0e^{u/q^2}$ with $\lambda_0=e^{2\pi ip/q}$. Then $e^{-2\pi i/\alpha}=\mu\,e^{\tilde u/p^2}$, where $\mu=e^{-2\pi iq/p}$ and
\[
\tilde u=\frac{u}{1+u/(2\pi i\,pq)}=u+\frac{i\,u^2}{2\pi pq}+O\Bigl(\frac{u^3}{q^2}\Bigr).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\alpha=(2\pi ipq+u)/(2\pi iq^2)$, so $-1/\alpha+q/p=qu/\bigl(p(2\pi ipq+u)\bigr)$, and $2\pi i p^2$ times this is $\tilde u$.
\end{proof}

Fix $p\ge1$ and $r$ with $\gcd(p,r)=1$, and let $q=pN+r$. Then $\mu=e^{-2\pi ir/p}$ does not depend on $N$. Let $\rho_{p,r}(u)$ be the multiplier of the $p$-cycle of
\[
\Mf_u(W)=\mu\,e^{u/p^2}\,\PP(W)
\]
that bifurcates from $W=0$, and write $\rho_{p,r}(u)=1-u+\kappa_{p,r}u^2+\rho_3u^3+\dots$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:index} and Remark~\ref{rem:general}, applied to the family $\mu e^{\varepsilon}\PP$ with $\varepsilon=u/p^2$, the first two coefficients are $1$ and $-1$ provided $(\mu\PP)^p(W)=W+aW^{p+1}+\dots$ with $a\ne0$; we verified $a\ne0$ numerically in all classes considered. Let $u_a$ be the endpoint of the arc $\rho_{p,r}^{-1}\bigl([-1,1)\bigr)$ that issues from $u=0$.

\begin{conjecture}[Renormalization principle]\label{conj:unfolding}
For fixed $(p,r)$ and $q=pN+r\to\infty$,
\[
R_{p/q}(u)=\rho_{p,r}\bigl(\tilde u(u)\bigr)+O(q^{-2})
\]
coefficientwise, and uniformly in $u$ on a neighbourhood of the arc from $0$ to $u_a$.
\end{conjecture}

Comparing coefficients gives the following consequences.

\begin{corollary}[conditional]\label{cor:kinematic}
Under Conjecture~\ref{conj:unfolding},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kinematic}
\kappa(p/q)=\kappa_{p,r}-\frac{i}{2\pi pq}+O(q^{-2}),\qquad r_3(p/q)=\rho_3+\frac{i\,\kappa_{p,r}}{\pi pq}+O(q^{-2}),
\end{equation}
where, by Theorem~\ref{thm:index} applied to $\mu e^\varepsilon\PP$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundedp}
\kappa_{p,r}=\frac{\iota\bigl((\mu\PP)^p\bigr)-\frac12}{p}.
\end{equation}
Moreover $G(p/q)\to\frac12|u_a|$, by Hurwitz's theorem and Definition~\ref{def:G}, provided $\rho_{p,r}'(u_a)\ne0$.
\end{corollary}

In particular the first-order term $-i/(2\pi pq)$ involves no property of the germ, which explains why it is the same in degree three (Section~\ref{sec:degree3}). We now test the three parts of Conjecture~\ref{conj:unfolding}: the limits of $\kappa$, the whole unfolding, and the first-order correction.

\subsection{Bounded $p$}\label{sec:boundedp}

\subsection{The first-order correction}\label{sec:kinematic}
The first identity in \eqref{eq:kinematic} is the coefficient $\gamma_1=-i/(2\pi)$ of Numerical result~\ref{obs:limit}. For $2\le p\le5$ the fitted first-order coefficients satisfy $\gamma_1(p)\cdot2\pi p/(-i)=1.0008,1.0011,1.0014,1.0016$; these fits are much less constrained than for $p=1$, and we give no error bars for them. The following test uses all Taylor coefficients at once and involves no fitting.

\begin{observation}\label{obs:kinematic}
Let $r_k(p/q)$ be the Taylor coefficients of $R_{p/q}$ and $\rho_k$ those of $\rho_{p,r}$, both computed by Cauchy integrals on $|u|=1.5$. For $2\le k\le8$, for $(p,r)=(1,1)$, $(2,1)$, $(3,1)$ and $(3,2)$, and for $N=64,128,\dots,1024$, the residuals $|r_k(p/q)-[u^k]\rho_{p,r}(\tilde u)|$ decrease by a factor between $3.77$ and $4.28$ per doubling of $N$. This holds in all $41$ cases in which the residual exceeds $10^{-8}$; below that level the round-off of the Cauchy integrals takes over. Without the substitution $u\mapsto\tilde u$ the factor lies between $1.1$ and $2.0$. In degree three, with the cubic horn germ, Cauchy integrals on $|u|=1$ and $p=1$, the factor lies between $3.75$ and $4.06$ ($26$ cases). Figure~\ref{fig:kinematic} shows the case $p=1$.
\end{observation}

\section{The hierarchy and the constant $\kappa_*$}\label{sec:hierarchy}

\subsection{The iteration}
For $\alpha=[0;m_1,m_2,\ldots]$ the renormalization $\mathcal R\fa{\alpha}$ has multiplier $e^{-2\pi i/\alpha}$. Conjugating by $z\mapsto\bar z$ turns its rotation number into $1/\alpha\bmod1=[0;m_2,m_3,\ldots]$, so that $\mathcal R$ acts on continued fractions as the Gauss map~\cite{IS}. When the first $k$ partial quotients tend to infinity, each renormalization should therefore be close to $\mathcal P$ applied to the complex conjugate of the previous one. Write $\bar\cG(W)=\overline{\cG(\bar W)}$, normalize germs to the form $v+v^2+O(v^3)$, and set
\[
\cG_1=\mathcal P(f_1),\qquad \cG_{k+1}=\mathcal P(\bar\cG_k),\qquad \kappa_k=\iota(\cG_k)-\tfrac12 .
\]
By Lemma~\ref{thm:horn-index}, $\kappa_k=a_2/(2\pi ia_1^2)$ for the horn map of $\bar\cG_{k-1}$, and $\kappa_1=\kappa_0$. For $k\ge2$ the germs $\cG_k$ are given by convergent series of small radius (about $2.6$ in the normalized coordinate $v$ at $k=2$), and the horn maps must be sampled at heights where the orbits stay well inside the disc of convergence. Table~\ref{tab:levels} lists $\kappa_1,\ldots,\kappa_7$ for two independent settings of height, Fourier truncation and precision. They agree to $2\cdot10^{-14}$ or better at every level.

\begin{observation}\label{obs:kstar}
The differences $\Delta_k=\kappa_k-\kappa_{k-1}$ decrease geometrically: $|\Delta_{k+1}|/|\Delta_k|$ tends to $0.0419$, and the ratios are $\Delta_{k+1}/\Delta_k\approx-0.01546\mp0.03894\,i$, with imaginary parts of alternating sign (Figure~\ref{fig:hierarchy}). This is the signature of an antilinear contraction $\Delta\mapsto\nu\bar\Delta$, as expected since the linearization of $\cG\mapsto\mathcal P(\bar\cG)$ is antilinear. Fitting $\Delta_{k+1}=L\Delta_k$ with $L$ real-linear on $\C\cong\R^2$ gives eigenvalues $\pm0.041899$ and the limit
\[
\kappa_*:=\lim_{k\to\infty}\kappa_k=0.011929183413-0.037455493752\,i,
\]
with the fits through levels $6$ and $7$ differing by less than $2\cdot10^{-12}$. The first Fourier coefficient $a_1$ converges in the same way, to $0.0409914-0.0089033\,i$, which is consistent with convergence of $\cG_k$ to a fixed point $\cG_*=\mathcal P(\bar\cG_*)$. If $\cG_*$ exists, its cubic coefficient is $\kappa_*+\frac12=0.5119292-0.0374555\,i$; we have not proved that it exists.
\end{observation}

\begin{remark}[The holomorphic operator]\label{rem:LY}
Without the conjugation, $\cG\mapsto\mathcal P(\cG)$ is the parabolic renormalization operator whose fixed point $f_*$ was shown to exist by Inou and Shishikura and computed by Lanford and Yampolsky~\cite{LY}. They report $f_*(z)\approx z+z^2+(0.514-0.0346\,i)z^3$ and a leading eigenvalue $\approx-0.017+0.040\,i$ of $D\mathcal P$ at $f_*$. Our code without conjugation reproduces both: the cubic coefficients converge to $0.5142768-0.0346276\,i$ (extrapolated, $\pm10^{-7}$), and the ratio of successive differences tends to $-0.01764+0.04038\,i$. The bulb data at the levels $k\le3$ follow the conjugated operator instead, whose limit $\kappa_*$ differs from the cubic coefficient of $f_*$ minus $\frac12$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Comparison with bulbs}
\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:hierarchy}
For every $k\ge1$,
\[
\lim_{N_k\to\infty}\cdots\lim_{N_1\to\infty}\kappa\bigl([0;N_1,\ldots,N_k]\bigr)=\begin{cases}\kappa_k,&k\text{ odd},\\ \overline{\kappa_k},&k\text{ even}.\end{cases}
\]
In particular $\kappa_*$ and $\overline{\kappa_*}$ are the limits of the first correction as the number of large partial quotients of $p/q$ grows, along odd and even $k$ respectively.
\end{conjecture}

The case $k=1$ is Numerical result~\ref{obs:identity}. For $k=2$, formula \eqref{eq:boundedp} with $r=1$ gives the limit of $\kappa([0;N,p])$ as $N\to\infty$ as an index of $(e^{-2\pi i/p}\PP)^p$. We computed this index for $p\le256$ with the normal form of Theorem~\ref{thm:nf} generalized to arbitrary germs, an $O(p^3)$ recursion, and extrapolated in $1/p$. The result, $0.0112359330+0.0377814233\,i$, equals $\overline{\kappa_2}$ to $1.2\cdot10^{-8}$ on the best fit; fits of lower order differ from it by up to $8\cdot10^{-7}$. The conjugate appears because $p^{-1}\bmod q\to0^-$ along this sequence, against $0^+$ for $p=1$. Without the conjugation in the definition of $\cG_2$ one obtains $0.014770-0.033911\,i$, which the data exclude.

We also tested the diagonal sequences $[0;N,\ldots,N]$, computing $\kappa$ with the normal form at $128$ and $192$ bits (the two agree to $4\cdot10^{-35}$) and fitting polynomials in $1/N$. For $k=2$ and $16\le N\le160$ ($q\le25601$) the limit agrees with $\overline{\kappa_2}$ to $3\cdot10^{-10}$, so the diagonal and the iterated limits coincide. For $k=3$ and $6\le N\le40$ ($q\le64080$) the best fit ($N\ge14$, degree $J=6$) gives $0.0119509937-0.0374804294\,i$, which agrees with $\kappa_3$ to $1.1\cdot10^{-6}$. The fits of degree $J=3,4,5,6$ on $N\ge14$ lie at distances $8\cdot10^{-5}$, $2.6\cdot10^{-5}$, $4.5\cdot10^{-6}$ and $1.1\cdot10^{-6}$ from $\kappa_3$, so we take $10^{-5}$ as the uncertainty. For comparison, $|\kappa_3-\kappa_2|=7.8\cdot10^{-4}$ and $|\kappa_3-\overline{\kappa_3}|=7.5\cdot10^{-2}$. Thus Conjecture~\ref{conj:hierarchy}, including its parity rule, holds numerically for $k\le3$. Level $4$, where $|\kappa_4-\kappa_3|=3.3\cdot10^{-5}$, would require periods $q\approx N^4$ with $N\gtrsim30$.

\section{Limits at the roots of satellites}\label{sec:component}

\subsection{One-sided limits at rationals}\label{sec:rational}
Let $p/q=[0;a_1,\ldots,a_n]$ be in lowest terms, and let $t$ be a finite tail of partial quotients ($t=\varnothing$ allowed). As $N\to\infty$ the satellites $[0;a_1,\ldots,a_n,N,t]$ of the cardioid accumulate at the root $c(e^{2\pi ip/q})$ of $W_{p/q}$: from above if $n$ is even and from below if $n$ is odd, in terms of the internal angle. For $p/q=0/1$ ($n=0$) these are the bounded-$p$ sequences of Section~\ref{sec:renorm}, since $[0;N,t]=p_t/(p_tN+r_t)$ with $r_t/p_t=[0;t]$ (and $p_t=r_t=1$ for $t=\varnothing$). The renormalization picture suggests that at the root of $W_{p/q}$ the cusp germ $z+z^2$ is replaced by $f_{p/q}(z):=e^{2\pi ip/q}z+z^2$, a parabolic germ with $q$ petals.

For $g=f_{p/q}^q=z+az^{q+1}+O(z^{q+2})$ the argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:fatou2} below applies verbatim: there is a unique formal Fatou coordinate
\[
\Phi(z)=-\frac1{qa}\,z^{-q}+\sum_{\substack{-q<k\le K\\k\ne0}}s_kz^k+\beta\log z+\dots,\qquad\Phi\circ g=\Phi+1,
\]
without constant term, since the coefficient of $z^{k+q}$ in $\Phi\circ g-\Phi$ contains $s_k$ with the factor $ka$ and that of $z^q$ contains $\beta$ with the factor $a$. Its logarithmic coefficient is $\beta=\resit(g)=\frac{q+1}2-\iota_{p/q}$ (we checked this numerically for $p/q=1/3$, $1/4$ and $2/5$). Let $P_0$ be an attracting petal of $g$ and $R_0$ the repelling petal that follows it counterclockwise. The $g$-orbits of points of $R_0$ at height $\operatorname{Im}Z$ in the repelling Fatou coordinate reach the attracting petal that follows $R_0$ counterclockwise when $\operatorname{Im}Z$ is above a bounded band, and $P_0$ when it is below; within the band they may escape. We determine the band numerically, and it is not centred at the naive value, because the terms $z^{-k}$, $k<q$, of $\Phi$ have imaginary parts on $R_0$ that depend on the depth. An orbit that lands in the $j$-th petal is carried back to $P_0$ by $f_{p/q}^m$ with $m\equiv-jp^{-1}\pmod q$, which is a step of $m/q$ in Fatou coordinates. This defines an upper horn map $E^+_{p/q}(Z)=Z+a_0+\sum_{n\ge1}a_ne^{2\pi inZ}$ ($\operatorname{Im}Z\to+\infty$) and a lower horn map $E^-_{p/q}(Z)=Z+b_0+\sum_{n\ge1}b_ne^{-2\pi inZ}$ ($\operatorname{Im}Z\to-\infty$), with parabolic germs $\mathcal P^+_{p/q}(W)=W\exp(2\pi i\sum a_nW^n)$ in $W=e^{2\pi iZ}$ and $\mathcal P^-_{p/q}(W)=W\exp(-2\pi i\sum b_nW^n)$ in $W=e^{-2\pi iZ}$. By Lemma~\ref{thm:horn-index},
\[
\iota(\mathcal P^+_{p/q})-\tfrac12=\frac{a_2}{2\pi ia_1^2},\qquad\iota(\mathcal P^-_{p/q})-\tfrac12=-\frac{b_2}{2\pi ib_1^2}.
\]
For $p/q=0/1$ this is $\PP$ and $\kappa_0$ (and its conjugate for the lower map). For $q\le2$ the germ $f_{p/q}$ is real, and the two maps are complex conjugate; for $q\ge3$ they are unrelated.

\begin{conjecture}[Root limits]\label{conj:rational}
With $\mathcal P^+_{p/q}$ if $n$ is even and $\mathcal P^-_{p/q}$ if $n$ is odd, and $\mu_t=e^{-2\pi ir_t/p_t}$,
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\kappa\bigl([0;a_1,\ldots,a_n,N,t]\bigr)=\frac{\iota\bigl((\mu_t\mathcal P^\pm_{p/q})^{p_t}\bigr)-\frac12}{p_t}.
\]
In particular, $\kappa$ has at every rational $p/q$ the one-sided limits $\iota(\mathcal P^\pm_{p/q})-\frac12$ along $t=\varnothing$.
\end{conjecture}

For $p/q=0/1$ this is Conjecture~\ref{conj:main} together with \eqref{eq:boundedp}.

Table~\ref{tab:rational} compares Conjecture~\ref{conj:rational} with the bulbs for $p/q=1/3$, $1/4$, $2/5$, $3/7$ from both sides, and at $1/3$ also for the tails $t=2$ (both sides) and $t=3$. The bulb values are normal-form values for $N=128,\dots,2048$, extrapolated by polynomials of degree four in $1/q$; changing the degree to three moves them by at most $1.3\cdot10^{-9}$. All eleven limits agree with the horn maps to within $6\cdot10^{-11}$.
\end{observation}

\subsection{The period-two disc}
Conjecture~\ref{conj:rational} concerns the satellites of the cardioid near the root of $W_{p/q}$. The satellites of $W_{p/q}$ itself accumulate at the same point, and Remark~\ref{rem:general} applies to them. For the period-two disc $W_{1/2}$, $c=-1+\lambda/4$, with $\lambda$ the multiplier of the attracting $2$-cycle, we write $\kappa_{W_{1/2}}(p/q)$ for the coefficient of \eqref{eq:R}. At generic $p/q$ it is close to the value of the cardioid at the same $p/q$: for $p/q=[0;2,9,4,256]$, $[0;3,5,256]$ and $[0;5,1,2,256]$ the two differ by $3.8\cdot10^{-6}$, $3.7\cdot10^{-5}$ and $7.8\cdot10^{-5}$. The bounded-$p$ limits, however, differ from those of the cardioid; for instance $\lim_{q\to\infty}\kappa_{W_{1/2}}(1/q)\ne\kappa_0$. The following conjecture attributes them to the germ at the root.

\begin{conjecture}[Root principle]\label{conj:root}
The limits of $\kappa$ and of $G$ along satellites $[0;N,t]$ of a hyperbolic component, as $N\to\infty$ with the tail $t$ fixed, depend only on the parabolic germ at the root of the component. In particular the satellite $[0;N,t]$ of $W_{1/2}$ and the satellite $[0;1,1,N-1,t]$ of the cardioid, which is governed by $\mathcal P^+_{1/2}$ (Conjecture~\ref{conj:rational}), have the same limits.
\end{conjecture}

Here $[0;N,t]\mapsto[0;1,1,N-1,t]$ is the map $x\mapsto1/(2-x)$. Table~\ref{tab:diskroot} confirms the second statement for three tails, and for the bulb size. The best fits agree to about $10^{-9}$; changing the degree of the fit from two to three moves the limits by at most $1.3\cdot10^{-7}$, which we take as the uncertainty. The disc values come from Cauchy integrals in the multiplier of the $2$-cycle, and the cardioid values from the normal form. When the first partial quotient stays bounded the correspondence fails: at $N=256$ the two sides differ by between $3\cdot10^{-5}$ and $1.5\cdot10^{-3}$ for the prefixes $(2,9,4)$, $(3,5)$, $(1,4)$, $(5,1,2)$ and $(7)$, and the disc is then closer to the cardioid at the same $p/q$. This is consistent with the picture in which only the first renormalization, and hence the germ at the root, distinguishes components.

\subsection{The $\frac12$-root in exact arithmetic}
At $c=-\frac34$ the map is conjugate to $f(z)=-z+z^2$, and $g=f\circ f=z-2z^3+z^4$ has a fixed point of multiplicity three at $0$. Its attracting petals lie along $\pm\R$ and its repelling petals along $\pm i\R$, and $f$ exchanges the members of each pair. In this case the Fatou coordinate has rational coefficients.

\begin{lemma}[Two-petal Fatou coordinate]\label{lem:fatou2}
There is a unique formal series
\[
\Phi(z)=\tfrac14z^{-2}+s_{-1}z^{-1}+\beta\log z+\sum_{k\ge1}s_kz^k
\]
without constant term such that $\Phi\circ g=\Phi+1$. Its coefficients are rational: $s_{-1}=\frac14$, $\beta=\frac{11}8$, $s_1=-\frac5{16}$, $s_2=\frac{75}{64}$, $\ldots$ The coefficient $\beta$ is the r\'esidu it\'eratif $\frac32-\iota(g)$ of $g$, and $\iota(g)=\iota_{1/2}=\frac18$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $g(z)=z(1+h)$ with $h=-2z^2+z^3$. Then $\Phi\circ g-\Phi=\sum_ks_kz^k\bigl((1+h)^k-1\bigr)+\beta\log(1+h)$ (with $s_{-2}=\frac14$), and $z^k((1+h)^k-1)=-2kz^{k+2}+O(z^{k+3})$. The coefficient of $z^m$ therefore contains $s_{m-2}$ with the factor $-2(m-2)$ for $m\ne2$, and $\beta$ with the factor $-2$ for $m=2$, together with unknowns of lower index. Solving order by order gives existence and uniqueness, the constant term being free, and the stated values. The index is $\Res\,dz/(2z^3-z^4)=\frac18$, which agrees with $\iota_{1/2}$ in Remark~\ref{rem:exact}.
\end{proof}

In the construction of Section~\ref{sec:rational}, orbits leaving the repelling petal $+i\R$ above the escape band (which lies between the heights $1.5$ and $3$) reach the attracting petal $-\R$, and $f$, a half step of $g$, carries them to $+\R$. We write $\Ph=\mathcal P^+_{1/2}$ and $\kappa_{1/2}:=\iota(\Ph)-\frac12$.

\begin{observation}\label{obs:half}
Computed at the heights $4.5$ and $5.5$ with $100$ digits, which agree in all digits shown,
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{Re}\kappa_{1/2}&=\phantom{-}0.018405261617964781008961965439681,\\
\operatorname{Im}\kappa_{1/2}&=-0.042903723880230475207353337798906.
\end{align*}
This agrees with the bulb limits of Table~\ref{tab:diskroot} within their uncertainty of about $10^{-7}$; the best fits differ from it by $3\cdot10^{-10}$ for the disc and $2\cdot10^{-9}$ for the cardioid. The germ $\Ph$ also carries the rest of the theory for the disc. With the phase $e^{-2\pi ir/p}$ and $r=1$ (since $[0;N,t]=t/(tN+1)$), formula \eqref{eq:boundedp} gives $0.04755255819-0.01974956104\,i$ for $t=2$ and $0.05330701191-0.00260595580\,i$ for $t=3$. The unfolding $e^{u}\Ph$ gives $G=1.0201625875$ for $p=1$, and $-e^{u/4}\Ph$ gives $G=1.1070111364$ for $p=2$, against $1.107011147$ for disc bulbs along $2/(2N+1)$.
\end{observation}

The first-order coefficient of the disc, $\kappa_{W_{1/2}}(1/q)=\kappa_{1/2}+(-0.17743-0.19381\,i)/q+O(q^{-2})$, is not the kinematic value $-i/(2\pi q)$ of Corollary~\ref{cor:kinematic}. Proposition~\ref{prop:kinematic} concerns the multiplier whose rotation number is renormalized. At $c=-\frac34$ the natural candidate for that role is the multiplier $\nu$ of the fixed point of $f$, not the multiplier $\lambda=e^{s}$ of the $2$-cycle, and $\log(-\nu)=-\frac s4-\frac{11}{64}s^2-\frac{51}{512}s^3+\dots$; note that $\frac{11}{64}=\beta/8$. The kinematics of the disc therefore differ from those of the cardioid already at first order. Deriving the coefficient $-0.17743-0.19381\,i$ from this is an open problem.

\section{Degree three: a second universality class}\label{sec:degree3}

The main component of $z^3+c$ has two cusps, $c=\pm2/(3\sqrt3)$, exchanged by $c\mapsto-c$. Near $c_*=2/(3\sqrt3)$ we parametrize it by the multiplier $\lambda$ of the fixed point $z=+(\lambda/3)^{1/2}$, with $c=z-z^3$; the map $\lambda\mapsto c$ is two-to-one on the whole component, and we use this branch. At $\lambda=1$, $z_*=1/\sqrt3$, and the parabolic germ is $w\mapsto w+\sqrt3\,w^2+w^3$. In $v=\sqrt3\,w$ this is
\[
g(v)=v+v^2+\tfrac13v^3,
\]
whose r\'esidu it\'eratif is $1-\frac13=\frac23$. Lemma~\ref{lem:fatou} generalizes with $F(w)=-1/g(-1/w)$ and $\Phi(w)=w-\frac23\log w+\sum c_kw^{-k}$, where $c_1,c_2,\ldots=\frac29,\frac5{54},\frac{14}{243},\frac{89}{2430},\ldots$. The horn map gives, at 50 and at 70 digits,
\[
\kappa_0^{(3)}:=\frac{a_2}{2\pi ia_1^2}=0.143599278095291569488064104088-0.030311647898502537611943110858\,i .
\]
Near-parabolic renormalization for unicritical maps of this degree was developed by Ch\'eritat~\cite{Cheritat} and Yang~\cite{Yang}, and the heuristics of Section~\ref{sec:renorm} apply with the cusp germ. Write $\kappa^{(3)}(p/q)$ for the coefficient of \eqref{eq:R} in this family. Table~\ref{tab:deg3} shows that $\kappa^{(3)}(1/q)+\frac i{2\pi q}$ converges to $\kappa_0^{(3)}$ like $q^{-2}$. The correction $-i/(2\pi q)$ is thus the same as in degree two, as Corollary~\ref{cor:kinematic} predicts, while the constant is not. The bounded-$p$ predictions hold in degree three as well: $\kappa$ agrees to within $5\cdot10^{-6}$ for $(p,r)=(1,1),(2,1),(3,1),(3,2)$ at $q\approx512p$.

The bulb size needs one more observation.

The following statement is classical~\cite{MilnorHC}; we include a short proof, since we use its last part.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:antipodes}
Let $W$ be a hyperbolic component of $z^d+c$, of period $n$. Its multiplier map $\rho:W\to\D$ has the form $\rho=\phi^{\,d-1}$ with $\phi:W\to\D$ a conformal isomorphism. Consequently $\rho^{-1}\bigl((-1,0]\bigr)$ consists of $d-1$ arcs issuing from the centre, and each of them converges to a single point of $\partial W$ at which the cycle has multiplier $-1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The map $\rho$ is proper, since the cycle becomes indifferent at $\partial W$. Its zeros are the parameters at which the critical point lies on the cycle, and there is exactly one, the centre $c_0$, because $W$ is a simply connected domain with a unique centre~\cite{MilnorHC}. Let $P_n(c)=f_c^n(0)$. Then $P_1=c$ and $P_{k+1}'=dP_k^{d-1}P_k'+1$, so $P_n'\equiv1\pmod d$ as a polynomial with integer coefficients, and $P_n'(c_0)\ne0$ because $1/d$ is not an algebraic integer (Gleason's argument). Near $c_0$ the cycle point $z_0(c)$ close to $0$ solves $f_c^n(z)=z$, and $\partial_z(f_c^n(z)-z)=-1$ at $(c_0,0)$; by the implicit function theorem $z_0$ is holomorphic with $z_0'(c_0)=P_n'(c_0)\ne0$. Since $\rho=d^n\prod_jz_j^{d-1}$ and the other cycle points do not vanish at $c_0$, the map $\rho$ vanishes to order exactly $d-1$ at $c_0$. Composing with a Riemann map of $W$, we obtain a finite Blaschke product of degree $d-1$ with a single zero of order $d-1$, which is the $(d-1)$-st power of a M\"obius transformation. For the last statement: along each arc $|\rho|\to1$ and $\rho\to-1$, so the limit set of the arc in $\partial W$ is connected and consists of parameters at which $f_c$ has a cycle of period dividing $n$ with multiplier $-1$. These parameters are the zeros of a polynomial in $c$ that does not vanish at $c_0$, so they are finitely many, and the limit set is a point.
\end{proof}

In degree three we therefore define two bulb sizes $G_\pm$ by Definition~\ref{def:G}, one at each of the two landing points, and both are predicted. For $(p,r)=(1,1)$, $(3,1)$ and $(3,2)$ the predictions agree with the extrapolated bulb values to within $3\cdot10^{-5}$; for instance at $(3,1)$ the horn map gives $1.196074$ and $1.227092$, against $1.196075$ and $1.227091$. For $(2,1)$ the two landing points nearly coincide and their labels exchange as $N$ varies; the sum $G_++G_-$ agrees to $5\cdot10^{-6}$.

\section{Arithmetic nature}\label{sec:pslq}

For each of $\kappa_0$, $\kappa_0^{(3)}$ and $\kappa_{1/2}$ we searched with PSLQ~\cite{PSLQ}, at $28$ digits and coefficient bound $1000$, for integer relations between the real part, the imaginary part, the squared modulus or the argument divided by $\pi$, and every subset of at most three of $\{1,\pi,\pi^2,1/\pi,\log2,\zeta(3),\mathrm{Cat},\gamma\}$, where $\mathrm{Cat}$ is Catalan's constant. For $\kappa_0$ we also tried $\operatorname{Re}(2\pi i\kappa_0)$, $\operatorname{Im}(2\pi i\kappa_0)$, $|a_1|$ and $\arg a_1/\pi$. No relation was found. This is weak evidence, since the basis is small and omits, for instance, $e^{\pi}$ and $\Gamma(\frac14)$. It is consistent with the expectation that invariants of parabolic germs, such as the \'Ecalle--Voronin moduli~\cite{Ecalle,Voronin}, have no closed form, and we regard $\kappa_0$, $\kappa_0^{(3)}$ and $\kappa_{1/2}$ as new constants of this kind.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The first-order theory of the satellite bulbs is the Ford-circle picture, with radii $q^{-2}$ and Farey tangencies. The first correction, $\kappa(p/q)$, is the holomorphic index of a parabolic germ (Theorem~\ref{thm:index}), and it equals the index of the rotation quotient minus $\frac12$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:quotient}). Numerically it is controlled by near-parabolic renormalization. The constant $\kappa_0$ comes from the simplest instance, $\alpha=1/q\to0$. Bounded $p$ corresponds to one renormalization step with phase $e^{-2\pi ir/p}$, and the first order in $1/q$ comes from the exact M\"obius phase. Large partial quotients correspond to iterated renormalization, which contracts by a factor $0.042$ per step towards a limit that differs from the one given by the fixed point of Lanford and Yampolsky. Hyperbolicity of $\mathcal R$ would explain the observation, reported in~\cite{repo}, that $\kappa(p/q)$ depends mainly on the last partial quotients, that is, on $p^{-1}\bmod q$. Finally, every rational $p/q$ carries a pair of such constants: the one-sided limits of $\kappa$ at $p/q$ are the indices of the upper and lower parabolic renormalizations of $e^{2\pi ip/q}z+z^2$, and for the period-two disc the component enters only through this germ at its root. Farey-type combinatorics survives at every order, while the metric data at a rational are, conjecturally, those of the parabolic germ there.

\subsection*{Open problems}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Prove Conjecture~\ref{conj:main}, for instance by carrying out steps (a) and (b) of Section~\ref{sec:renorm}.
\item Prove Conjecture~\ref{conj:unfolding}, in particular that the renormalized germ has no $O(1/q)$ correction beyond the phase of Proposition~\ref{prop:kinematic}. By Corollary~\ref{cor:kinematic} this gives the universal term $-i/(2\pi pq)$. Identify the $O(q^{-2})$ term.
\item Prove Conjecture~\ref{conj:hierarchy}, prove that the fixed point $\cG_*$ of $\cG\mapsto\mathcal P(\bar\cG)$ exists, and describe it and its spectrum.
\item Compute the generic limit function $\kappa(p/q)$, $p\to\infty$, by iterated renormalization with bounded partial quotients, including its values at quadratic irrationals as periodic points of $\mathcal R$.
\item Prove Conjecture~\ref{conj:rational}; its case $p/q=0/1$ is Conjecture~\ref{conj:main}. Relate the jump $\iota(\mathcal P^+_{p/q})-\iota(\mathcal P^-_{p/q})$ to the arithmetic of $p/q$.
\item Prove Conjecture~\ref{conj:root}, test it on components of higher period (whose roots have $q\ge3$ petals and distinct upper and lower germs), and derive the first-order coefficient $-0.17743-0.19381\,i$ of the disc from the kinematics at $c=-\frac34$ (Section~\ref{sec:component}).
\item Decide whether $\kappa_0$ can be expressed in closed form through the \'Ecalle--Voronin invariants of $z+z^2$.
\end{enumerate}

\appendix
\section{Numerical methods and error control}\label{app:num}

\subsection*{A.1 Normal form}
Recursion \eqref{eq:rec} was run in \texttt{mpmath}~\cite{mpmath} at $50$ working digits for $64\le q\le8192$ ($p=1$) and for the bounded-$p$ classes of Table~\ref{tab:boundedp} ($q\le5124$). It was also implemented in Arb ball arithmetic~\cite{Arb}. There the online convolution $(H^2)_k$ is evaluated in blocks: completed blocks are folded in by truncated series products, and the remainder is summed directly. The working precision is doubled until the certified radius of $\kappa$ is below $10^{-40}$. For $p=1$ the radii are $1.6\cdot10^{-50}$ at $q=1024$ and below $2\cdot10^{-111}$ at $q=32768$, and the two implementations agree to the $40$ digits stored. The ball version is $60$--$110$ times faster; $q=32768$ takes about two minutes. For generic $p$ the coefficients $H_k$ are very large and ball radii, which cannot see cancellation, grow until they carry no information. For those values we ran the same code in midpoint arithmetic at two precisions, $128$ and $192$ bits, and took their difference as the error; it never exceeded $4\cdot10^{-35}$. The recursion for general germs used in Section~\ref{sec:hierarchy} was run in \texttt{mpmath} at $40$--$60$ digits.

\subsection*{A.2 Extrapolation}
Limits in $q$ or $N$ come from least-squares fits by polynomials of degree $J$ in $1/q$ or $1/N$, solved by QR decomposition at $40$--$80$ digits. We quote the best overdetermined fit and, as its uncertainty, the spread of the fits that remain stable under changes of $J$ and of the smallest $q$ used. The limit of the hierarchy (Numerical result~\ref{obs:kstar}) uses a real-linear model $\Delta_{k+1}=L\Delta_k$ fitted to the last three differences. This model is exact for both linear and antilinear geometric contractions, whereas componentwise Aitken extrapolation is not exact for antilinear ones.

\subsection*{A.3 Horn maps}
For the cusp germs the Fatou series were truncated at $K=40$ terms and evaluated at $|w|\ge800$, where the truncation error is below $10^{-60}$; the coefficients $|c_k|$ grow roughly like $k!/(2\pi)^k$. We used $n=1000$--$1800$ repelling iterations and computed Fourier coefficients from $64$--$80$ samples on $\operatorname{Im}Z=\frac14$, with aliasing below $10^{-58}$. For the germs $\cG_k$ of the hierarchy, which are known only as convergent series, the horn maps were sampled at $\operatorname{Im}Z=1$ and $\frac54$ so that all orbits stay well inside the disc of convergence. A first run at $\operatorname{Im}Z=\frac12$ was inaccurate at the $10^{-3}$ level and was discarded. For the germs $e^{2\pi ip/q}z+z^2$ of Section~\ref{sec:component} the Fatou series (exact for $q=2$, Lemma~\ref{lem:fatou2}; at working precision otherwise) was truncated at $K=30$--$60$, with $600$--$1500$ iterations of $g$. The escape band was located by scanning $\operatorname{Im}Z$ in steps of $\frac14$, and the samples were taken $1.5$ beyond it (at $4.5$--$5.5$ for $q=2$). The working precision includes the digits lost to the amplification $e^{2\pi M|h|}$, and the reproduction of $\kappa_0$, $\overline{\kappa_0}$ and $\kappa_{1/2}$ by the general code is part of the test suite. A Fourier coefficient $a_n$ extracted at height $h$ carries noise amplified by $e^{2\pi nh}$, and the working precision ($60$--$160$ digits) was chosen accordingly. Independent heights agree to all digits reported.

\subsection*{A.4 Multiplier data}
The Taylor coefficients $r_k$ were computed by Cauchy integrals of $R_{p/q}$ over a circle, continuing the $q$-cycle numerically, and those of the renormalized families in the same way. For $z^2+c$ radii up to $|u|=2.5$ are admissible. For $z^3+c$ the function $R_{p/q}$ has a singularity inside $|u|<2.5$, and the radius must be at most $1.5$. The condition $r_1=-1$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:index} is a reliable diagnostic: on the larger circle it fails, with $r_1=-1.032$. In degree three each satellite has two landing points of multiplier $-1$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:antipodes}), and both were reached by continuation of the target multiplier from $0$ to $-1$.

\subsection*{A.5 Reproducibility}
All code, data and tests are in the accompanying repository~\cite{repo}. The package \texttt{bulbford} contains the normal forms (\texttt{normal\_form}, \texttt{normal\_form\_ball}), the horn maps (\texttt{horn}, \texttt{horn\_half}), the germ and renormalization tools (\texttt{germ}, \texttt{renorm}), the extrapolation (\texttt{extrapolate}) and the dynamics (\texttt{dynamics}, whose cycle iteration is compiled with Numba). Every table and figure is generated from the data files by \texttt{paper/tables.py} and \texttt{paper/figures.py}. The test suite checks Theorems~\ref{thm:index} and~\ref{thm:nf}, the value of $\kappa_0$ to $25$ digits, the bounded-$p$, kinematic, hierarchy and degree-three predictions, and the constant $\kappa_{1/2}$.
